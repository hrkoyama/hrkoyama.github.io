Well-posedness, pure-Neumann compatibility, and uniqueness

Learning goal: Understand the arbitrary constant, integral compatibility, mean-value gauge, and boundary-inclusive stability of a pure-Neumann problem.

When only the normal derivative is prescribed at both ends, adding a constant does not change either derivative. A pure-Neumann Poisson solution is therefore undetermined by an additive constant. Moreover, no solution exists unless the source and boundary data balance.

Nonexistence and nonuniqueness are different problems. Check both before responding to a stalled calculation by merely increasing the iteration count.

Integrate to obtain compatibility

Integrating-u″=f over[0,1] requires the right-hand side and outward normal derivatives g_L,g_R to satisfy the balance below. Check the corresponding discrete balance too, with half weights at the endpoints.

Fix the mean

Even with compatibility, the matrix retains the constant-vector nullspace. Choose a gauge, such as zero mean, to select one solution for comparison. This selects the undetermined additive constant rather than arbitrarily changing physical data.

Include boundaries in stability considerations

The same interior operator can have different nullspaces and definiteness under different boundary conditions. Robin conditions with α,β>0 add positive endpoint terms to the discrete energy. Interior Fourier analysis alone does not settle every boundary treatment.

For the mean constraint in this chapter, w denotes dimensionless weights:1/2 at endpoints and 1 inside. The integration-norm weights in Chapter 2 include an additional factor h.

A small worked example

Choose u=cos(2πx), giving zero normal derivatives at both ends and f=4π²cos(2πx). The grid's trapezoidal weights also satisfy compatibility.

The code temporarily fixes one value to solve the linear system, then shifts the entire solution by a constant to obtain zero mean. It also checks the residual of the original boundary row that was omitted; satisfying the gauge alone is not enough.

from lab import poisson, neumann_compatibility
x, u, ref, residual = poisson(32, "neumann")
h = 1/32
mean = h*(sum(u)-(u[0]+u[-1])/2)
print(mean, residual)
# f=1, g_left=g_right=0 is incompatible

A dedicated check rejects an incompatible constant source. The exact +1 curve has the same Neumann data but is not selected by the zero-mean gauge.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: Does-u″=f have a solution with f=1 and zero normal derivatives at both endpoints?

Show answer

No. The left side of the integral compatibility condition is 1.

Question 2: Does compatibility alone give uniqueness?

Show answer

An arbitrary constant remains. Fix a mean value or another gauge to select one solution.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not treat a singular matrix as merely a nonsingular matrix with a large condition number.
  • Use the same mean-value convention for the reference and numerical solution.

Key points

  • Separate existence from uniqueness.
  • Check the source, boundary data, and gauge together.
