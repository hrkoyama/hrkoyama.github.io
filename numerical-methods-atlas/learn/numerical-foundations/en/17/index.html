Lie-Trotter and Strang splitting

Learning goal: Understand composition of subproblems and noncommutativity; distinguish first and second order under smooth conditions from boundary-induced order reduction.

When a right-hand side splits into two actions, it is tempting to solve each with a suitable method and compose the results. This is operator splitting. Even exact solutions of both subproblems need not compose into the exact solution of the original problem.

The cause is noncommutativity of the order. Use a small matrix or oscillator to compare advancing A then B with advancing in the reverse order.

Lie splitting: consecutive full substeps

Compose the partial flows φ_A and φ_B. In these formulas the rightmost map acts first. For a smooth finite-time problem with the required stability, Lie splitting usually has an O(h²) local defect and first-order global error.

Symmetric half-full-half composition

Strang splitting advances B by a half step, A by a full step, and B by a half step. For smooth problems its local defect is usually O(h³), giving second-order global error. Subsolver errors, stiffness, nonsmooth solutions, and boundary treatments can alter this order.

What changes when the operators commute?

For constant matrices satisfying AB=BA, composition of exact partial exponentials equals exp(h(A+B)). Thus examples using only commuting matrices hide the general ordering error. The code retains a commuting case as a control.

A small worked example

Use the unit-mass, unit-frequency oscillator q′=p, p′=-q. A kick changes only p and a drift only q, so each partial flow can be computed exactly.

A full kick followed by a full drift is the Lie step used here. A half kick, full drift, and half kick give Strang. Matching the initial state, final time T=1, and exact partial flows isolates the error due to composition order.

q, p, dt = 1.0, 0.0, 0.1
# Strang: kick / drift / kick
p -= 0.5*dt*q
q += dt*p
p -= 0.5*dt*q
print(q, p)

For 10,20,40,80 steps, Lie approaches first order and Strang second order. Both use exact partial flows in this comparison. Other partial solvers or boundary conditions cannot be substituted without rechecking the assumptions.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: If every subproblem is solved exactly, is their composition necessarily exact?

Show answer

Generally no when the flows do not commute. Splitting error due to ordering remains.

Question 2: Can the two Strang half steps be counted as extra physical time, doubling the final time?

Show answer

No. The complete composition approximates one physical step h.

Common pitfalls

  • Keep the reference, final time, and partial solvers the same when comparing Lie and Strang.
  • Do not treat formal splitting order as a guarantee for arbitrary stiff boundary-value problems.

Key points

  • State the composition order.
  • Separate noncommutativity from subproblem-solver error.
