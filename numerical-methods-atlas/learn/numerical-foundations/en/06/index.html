Absolute stability and stability regions

Learning goal: Use the test equation y′=λy and amplification function R(λΔt) to relate the time step to growth and decay.

First isolate a single decaying component. The test equation y′=λy has an exact exponential solution, which decays when Re λ<0. Examine how a time integrator treats this component one step at a time in the complex plane.

The one-step multiplier R(z) is the stability function. The product z=λΔt combines the problem's time scale with the selected step.

From amplification to a stability region

Apply a linear one-step method to the test equation. Its absolute stability region consists of z for which |R(z)|≤1. This prevents growth in the repeated test-equation update; it does not guarantee accurate phase or accurate values.

Three basic formulas

Forward Euler uses the slope at the old time, backward Euler uses the new time, and the trapezoidal rule averages the endpoint slopes. The formulas below describe the linear test equation. For a general nonlinear problem, backward Euler and the trapezoidal rule require solving equations.

Being inside the region does not eliminate error

The forward-Euler region is the disk centered at -1 with radius 1. On the negative real axis it gives -2≤z≤0. On a boundary where |R|=1, the numerical solution may not decay even when the exact solution does. Investigate accuracy separately by changing the step.

A small worked example

Take λ=-1 and T=1, with 10,20,40,80 steps. Compare each value R(-Δt)^N with exp(-1).

The forward- and backward-Euler errors are approximately first order, while the trapezoidal error is approximately second order. Size of the stability region and approximation order are separate properties.

from math import exp
N = 20
dt = 1.0/N
z = -dt
forward = (1+z)**N
backward = (1/(1-z))**N
trapezoidal = ((1+z/2)/(1-z/2))**N
print(exp(-1), forward, backward, trapezoidal)

For this fixed sequence, global error order is checked separately for all three methods. The colored regions depict stability in complex z, not measured error magnitude.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: What happens with λ=-10 and a forward-Euler step Δt=0.3?

Show answer

z=-3 gives R=-2, so the magnitude doubles each step.

Question 2: Does |R|≤1 imply that a coarse-step solution is close to the exact solution?

Show answer

No. Stability limits unwanted amplification; it is separate from an accuracy check.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not replace a complex λ by its real part when testing region membership.
  • Do not extend eigenvalue-only reasoning unconditionally to a general nonnormal matrix.

Key points

  • Compare λΔt with the stability region.
  • Measure accuracy and stability separately.
