The method of lines: separating space and time error

Learning goal: Distinguish the spatially discrete ODE from the fully discrete system, and design comparisons that vary time or space separately.

Discretizing only the space variables of a PDE produces an ODE with many unknowns. Integrating that ODE in time is the method of lines. Space and time methods can be selected separately, but both contribute error to the final result.

When reducing the time step, comparison against the continuous PDE and comparison against exact evolution of the fixed spatial discretization reveal different convergence behavior. Decide first which reference you intend to use.

Semidiscrete and fully discrete systems

Use centered spatial differences for the heat equation with Dirichlet endpoints. Let u_h(t) be the exact solution of the grid ODE and U its time-discrete approximation. The total error then separates into two terms.

A single sine mode provides a known reference

With u(x,0)=sin(πx) and zero Dirichlet data on[0,1], the grid sine vector is an eigenvector of the discrete matrix. The continuous and discrete decay rates provide separate references for temporal and total error.

Plan what to refine first

For temporal convergence, fix the spatial grid and compare with the semidiscrete reference. For spatial convergence, make temporal error sufficiently small or study total order along an explicitly stated coupled sequence. Errors can cancel; one unusually small total error is not sufficient evidence.

A small worked example

Fix κ=0.1, T=0.2, and N=40, and use 80,160,320,640 time steps. Compare forward Euler with the semidiscrete amplitude exp(λ_h T).

Compare the same numerical solution with the continuous amplitude exp(-κπ²T) as well. Temporal error can decrease at first order while spatial error and cancellation still influence the difference from the continuous reference.

from math import pi, sin, exp
N, kappa, T = 40, 0.1, 0.2
h = 1.0/N
lam_h = -4*kappa*sin(pi*h/2)**2/h**2
for steps in [80, 160, 320, 640]:
    dt = T/steps
    amplitude = (1+dt*lam_h)**steps
    print(dt, abs(amplitude-exp(lam_h*T)))

For the heat-grid sequence N=20,40,80, r=0.4 is held fixed and the total error under coupled space-time refinement is saved separately. Do not confuse that second-order sequence with first-order time convergence at fixed space.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: At fixed spatial grid, does Δt tending to 0 necessarily recover the continuous PDE solution?

Show answer

It approaches the fixed-grid semidiscrete solution. Spatial error remains.

Question 2: Is the semidiscrete reference an independent general PDE solver?

Show answer

No. It is an analytic reference obtained from the eigenvalue of a discrete sine mode.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not call coupled space-time refinement a measurement of temporal order alone.
  • Do not use cancellation of total error as evidence of high order.

Key points

  • State what is fixed and what is refined.
  • Specify the equation solved by the reference.
