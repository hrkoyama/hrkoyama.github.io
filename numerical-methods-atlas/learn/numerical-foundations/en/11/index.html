Numerical dispersion: phase speed, group speed, and waveforms

Learning goal: Use the argument of amplification to examine phase and propagation-speed error, understand waveform distortion without amplitude loss, and distinguish physical dispersion.

A waveform changes if its crests travel at the wrong speeds, even when amplitude is nearly preserved. Numerical dispersion is a wavenumber-dependent departure of phase evolution from the exact equation. Measure it separately from numerical dissipation.

Phase speed describes motion of a wave crest, while group speed describes an envelope occupying a narrow wavenumber band. One group speed does not describe every arbitrary broadband waveform.

Read the argument of a complex multiplier

With time dependence exp(-iωt), write G=|G|exp(iφ) and read the real numerical angular frequency as-φ/Δt. Choose a continuous phase branch; do not turn a jump from π to-π into a physical speed discontinuity.

Constant-speed advection has a constant reference speed

For exact constant-speed advection, both phase and group speed equal a. Using the discrete G expresses speed ratios in terms of θ. The figure uses 0<θ<π, avoiding the sign and branch ambiguity at the Nyquist endpoint.

Distinguish physical dispersion

If a medium or equation itself has wavenumber-dependent propagation speed, dispersion is physical. Our reference is nondispersive constant-speed advection, so deviations of the plotted ratios are numerical. Always show their dependence on steps and grid resolution.

A small worked example

Compute the argument of complex G to obtain the phase-speed ratio. Evaluate group speed from the derivative of G, and test it against a separate small-difference derivative of the phase.

The advection runs in this edition transport a sum of two sine waves. Group speeds in the figure are evaluated from the discrete symbol; they are not direct measurements tracking the center of a localized wave packet.

from cmath import phase
from lab import symbol
nu, theta = 0.6, 0.4
G = symbol("lax-wendroff", theta, nu)
phase_speed_ratio = -phase(G)/(nu*theta)
print(phase_speed_ratio)

At high wavenumbers, amplitude damping and phase error can both become large. Phase measurements of extremely weak components can also become unreliable, so read them together with |G|.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: Does |G|=1 guarantee accurate wave transport?

Show answer

No: an incorrect phase is still an error. Amplitude preservation and propagation-speed accuracy are separate.

Question 2: What can go wrong when directly differencing arg G?

Show answer

A branch jump from π to-π can produce an artificially huge derivative. Check continuity of the phase branch.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not assign the group-speed formula uniformly to an arbitrary broadband waveform.
  • Do not try to remove physical dispersion as if it were numerical error.

Key points

  • Measure phase and amplitude separately.
  • Relate speed at each wavenumber to its resolution.
