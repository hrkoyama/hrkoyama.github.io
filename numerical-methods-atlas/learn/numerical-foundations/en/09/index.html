CFL conditions and domains of dependence

Learning goal: Connect information speed to the reach of a stencil, and distinguish necessary from sufficient conditions.

Advection transports information at finite speed. The grid points available to a numerical solution must cover the range from which physical information arrives. Otherwise, the discretization cannot approximate the correct dependence. This idea motivates the CFL condition.

Covering the physical domain of dependence is different from preventing error amplification. In the appropriate setting, CFL is necessary; it is not a sufficient condition that stabilizes every difference scheme.

Advection and the upwind direction

For a>0, the exact advection solution is the initial waveform shifted to the right by a t. The upwind scheme uses the value to the left. For 0≤ν≤1, its new value is a convex combination of two old values. Reverse the direction when a<0.

An example where CFL is not sufficient

FTCS with centered spatial differences is unstable at ν=0.6 despite satisfying the domain-of-dependence requirement. Check amplification for the particular method. A large implicit stability region also does not restore temporal accuracy lost through coarse steps.

Derive diffusion restrictions separately

For the one-dimensional heat equation with centered space and forward Euler in time, r=κΔt/h²≤1/2. This explicit stability restriction follows from amplification factors or discrete eigenvalues. It is not obtained simply by reusing the finite-speed advection argument.

A small worked example

For upwind with ν=1.2, the coefficient 1-ν is negative. The figure compares this example with FTCS at ν=0.6. Both amplify, but their diagnoses differ.

The first violates the upwind stability condition; the second satisfies the necessary CFL condition but uses an unsuitable scheme. A mode with a four-grid-spacing period makes amplification easy to measure.

from math import sqrt
nu = 1.2
print("upwind, theta=pi:", abs(1-2*nu))
nu = 0.6
print("FTCS, theta=pi/2:", sqrt(1+nu*nu))

Common pitfalls

  • Do not carry the one-dimensional coefficient 1/2 unchanged into multiple dimensions.
  • Do not substitute implicit stability for temporal resolution of the model.

Key points

  • Distinguish advection and diffusion step restrictions.
  • Check method-specific stability as well as CFL.
