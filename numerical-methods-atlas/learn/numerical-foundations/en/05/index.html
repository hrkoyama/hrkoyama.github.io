Stability, convergence, and the Lax equivalence theorem

Learning goal: For a well-posed linear initial-value problem and a consistent linear approximation, relate uniform stability to convergence in a specified norm.

A formula may approximate the right differential equation and still fail to approach the reference solution if it amplifies small input errors severely. Consistency must be connected with stability to discuss convergence.

The Lax-Richtmyer equivalence theorem considered here concerns a well-posed linear initial-value problem and a consistent linear approximation. It does not say that consistency alone suffices for every numerical calculation.

A uniform bound over finite time

Let B_h advance the discrete solution, and consider a fixed finite time T. The powers of B_h that amplify initial error must be bounded by a constant independent of the grid spacing and time step. The chosen norm is part of the definition.

How to read the equivalence theorem

Within the setting of a well-posed linear problem and a consistent linear approximation, stability and convergence are equivalent. The estimate above shows the key direction from stability to convergence; a rigorous converse requires the function-space setting. Do not apply the result unchanged to nonlinear conservation laws or discontinuous solutions.

A small worked example

FTCS advection, combining centered spatial differences and forward Euler, is consistent for smooth solutions. However, at a fixed Courant number it amplifies some grid wavenumbers.

Refinement also increases the number of steps required to reach the same physical time. A mode with a fixed one-step amplification greater than 1 has unbounded powers along that sequence.

from math import sqrt
nu = 0.6
G_abs = sqrt(1+nu*nu)  # theta=pi/2
for n in [10, 20, 30]:
    print(n, G_abs**n)

The saved diagnostic detects amplification by FTCS even when the CFL condition is satisfied. Passing this diagnostic means detecting an intentionally unstable example; it does not qualify FTCS as a stable advection scheme.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: May the stability constant grow without bound as h decreases?

Show answer

Not in the Lax-Richtmyer sense. The estimate must be independent of the steps at fixed finite time T.

Question 2: Does this theorem alone establish convergence for a nonlinear problem containing shocks?

Show answer

No. The classes of problems, solutions, and discretizations differ, and require separate analysis.

Common pitfalls

  • Eigenvalues of one fixed matrix alone do not establish uniform stability for an entire grid family.
  • Do not call a few experiments a proof of the theorem.

Key points

  • Consider consistency and stability together.
  • Specify the norm, finite time, and uniformity.
