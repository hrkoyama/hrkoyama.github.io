A common verification procedure using small problems

Learning goal: Combine analytic and manufactured solutions, error norms, convergence, residuals, conserved quantities, energy budgets, and boundary error into one procedure.

Finally, assemble a small verification procedure usable across disciplines. A calculation that finishes and produces a smooth-looking figure has not necessarily solved the intended equation correctly.

Start with a small problem and choose inputs, reference, and acceptance criteria before running it. Preserve failed experiments; do not relax criteria afterward and relabel past failures as successes.

Choose the comparison reference

When an analytic solution is available, compare at the same variable locations and time. A manufactured solution begins with the desired solution and derives the matching source and boundary data. If the reference is numerical, examine its error and scope as well.

Ask several separate questions

Record error norms and order, algebraic residuals, conservation or energy budgets, and boundary, positivity, and phase behavior separately. One indicator cannot establish every requirement. Also distinguish theoretical stability from observations for a few fixed inputs.

Make a reproducible record

Bind code, inputs, environment, outputs, and criteria to the same edition. Produce figures from saved numerical tables, distinguishing schematic illustrations from measurements. Extract the ZIP independently and rerun its tests and examples. This verifies an implementation; it does not establish the physical validity of the model.

A small worked example

The calculations use only standard Python; regenerating figures requires separate plotting dependencies. The first coarse upwind sequence failed one order criterion. Its record is retained, and the sequence was refined one level without changing the criterion.

For unstable controls and low-order boundary examples, the criteria check whether the expected problem is detected. Do not read passed as meaning that every method is practically accurate.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: Is visual similarity of figures enough to complete verification?

Show answer

No. You need matched comparison conditions, errors, convergence, budgets, reference accuracy, and reproducible records.

Question 2: Does agreement with a manufactured solution prove that the physical model agrees with experiment?

Show answer

No. Checking that the intended equations are discretized and implemented correctly differs from validating the model itself.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not mix illustrative numbers with measured results.
  • Do not erase failures, relax tolerances, or relabel them as past successes.

Key points

  • Fix the problem, reference, and criteria before execution.
  • Use small examples to check several indicators and reproducibility.
