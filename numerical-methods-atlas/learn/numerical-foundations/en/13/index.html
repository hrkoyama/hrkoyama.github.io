Initial conditions and Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin boundaries

Learning goal: Understand prescribed values, outward normal derivatives, and linear combinations of the two.

A differential equation alone does not determine a unique solution. Initial conditions specify the starting state in time, and boundary conditions constrain the spatial edges. Both are part of the model and should not be chosen merely for computational convenience.

Elliptic problems such as Poisson and transport problems such as advection require different boundary information. For advection in particular, what is prescribed at inflow matters.

Three basic forms

Dirichlet specifies a value, Neumann an outward normal derivative, and Robin a linear combination. When Neumann data represent physical flux, state the coefficient and sign relation explicitly. A diffusion flux is conventionally-κ∂_n u. In the equations, g_D, g_N, and g_R denote prescribed Dirichlet, Neumann, and Robin data.

The outward-normal direction

On[0,1], the left normal is -1 and the right normal is +1. Thus ∂_n u is-u′(0) on the left and u′(1) on the right. Using the same +x derivative at both ends changes the boundary-flux balance.

Consider conditions together with the equation

For a time-dependent problem, initial and boundary data should also be compatible where they meet. Incompatibility can create initial layers or nonsmoothness, preventing smooth-solution orders from appearing directly. Periodic boundaries are a different condition that joins the endpoints.

A small worked example

Choose an exact solution of-u″=f first, then derive the right-hand side and boundary data. Use sin(πx)+0.3x +1 for Dirichlet, cos(2πx) for Neumann, and exp(x) for Robin.

These are separate manufactured solutions checking each boundary type, not a comparison that changes only the boundary condition while holding f fixed. Whenever the problem changes, use its matching reference.

from math import exp
alpha, beta = 2.0, 1.0
u_left, ux_left = 1.0, 1.0
u_right, ux_right = exp(1), exp(1)
g_left = alpha*u_left-beta*ux_left
g_right = alpha*u_right+beta*ux_right
print(g_left, g_right)

For the Robin example, g_left=1 and g_right=3e. Checking the signs alone can prevent serious boundary-treatment errors.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: Is the outward normal derivative at the left endpoint +u′(0)?

Show answer

No. On[0,1], it is-u′(0).

Question 2: Does a Neumann condition always specify the physical flux itself?

Show answer

No. Signs and coefficients can relate the normal derivative to physical flux. Check the definition.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not transfer elliptic boundary prescriptions unconditionally to advection.
  • Do not immediately blame the numerical method for incompatible initial and boundary data.

Key points

  • Boundary conditions are part of the problem.
  • State the signs of normals and fluxes explicitly.
