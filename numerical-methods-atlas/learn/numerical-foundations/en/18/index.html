Symplectic integration and long-time behavior

Learning goal: Introduce Hamiltonian phase space through the harmonic oscillator, and distinguish preservation of geometric structure from exact energy conservation.

For long computations of oscillations or orbits, examine the geometric structure preserved by time evolution as well as one-step accuracy. Symplectic integration preserves the canonical structure of a Hamiltonian system.

Symplecticity differs from exact conservation of the original energy at every step. It supports useful long-time behavior but does not guarantee an accurate orbit for every step size or system.

Phase space and canonical structure

Work in phase space, pairing position q with momentum p. With multiple degrees of freedom, the condition preserves the canonical two-form; volume preservation alone is insufficient. For a linear map with one degree of freedom, determinant 1 corresponds to the symplectic condition.

Two methods obtained by splitting

For the previous chapter's oscillator, kick then drift gives first-order symplectic Euler, while half kick, drift, and half kick give second-order Störmer-Verlet. Verlet is also time-symmetric; symplectic Euler is generally not.

Preserved quadratic forms and physical energy

For this unit oscillator at fixed step h, the modified quadratic forms below are conserved in exact arithmetic. They are positive definite for |h|<2, helping explain bounded physical-energy error. These simple quadratic expressions do not apply to general Hamiltonian systems.

A small worked example

Start at q(0)=1, p(0)=0 and take 6000 steps of h=0.1 to time 600. Forward Euler increases energy and backward Euler decreases it. Both introduce large errors for the purpose of sustaining Hamiltonian oscillations over long times.

Symplectic Euler and Verlet have oscillatory energy errors. Their measured maximum relative errors here are about 5.26% and 0.25%, respectively; the modified quadratic forms are preserved to roundoff. Yet both retain a final-time phase error of about 0.25 rad.

from lab import oscillator_step
q, p, h = 1.0, 0.0, 0.1
for _ in range(6000):
    q, p = oscillator_step(q, p, h, "verlet")
print(q, p, (q*q+p*p)/2)

Good energy behavior does not imply accurate phase. Do not transfer fixed-step geometric or long-time estimates unchanged to ordinary state-dependent step changes. This course does not verify guarantees for general nonlinear Hamiltonian systems or adaptive steps.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: Does symplecticity make H exactly the same at every step?

Show answer

Generally no. Preserving canonical structure differs from exact conservation of the original Hamiltonian.

Question 2: For this oscillator, does a small energy error ensure correct phase?

Show answer

No. The measured long-time phase error remains, so examine the orbit and phase separately.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not replace one-degree-of-freedom area preservation with a volume-only condition for multiple degrees of freedom.
  • Do not treat A/L-stability and Hamiltonian structure preservation as the same goal.

Key points

  • State what the numerical map preserves.
  • Separate energy, phase, and time symmetry.
