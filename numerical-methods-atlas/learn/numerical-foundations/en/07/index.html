A-stability, L-stability, and stiff problems

Learning goal: Separate stability over the left half-plane from damping of very stiff modes; accuracy remains a separate question.

Diffusion on a fine grid contains components that decay very quickly alongside components that change slowly. Stability of the fast components can require steps much smaller than the slow evolution of interest. This is an entry point to understanding stiff problems.

A-stability includes the whole left half-plane of decaying linear components in the stability region. L-stability additionally requires sufficiently strong damping of extremely stiff components. Distinguish these two requirements.

A-stability

A method is A-stable when R is defined and |R(z)|≤1 for every z with Re z≤0. Backward Euler and the trapezoidal rule are A-stable. Forward Euler has a bounded stability region and is not A-stable. This classification concerns the linear test equation.

L-stability

L-stability additionally requires R(z) to tend to 0 as |z| grows within the left half-plane. Backward Euler satisfies this condition. For the trapezoidal rule, R tends to -1 along the negative real axis, so a component that should rapidly disappear can persist with alternating sign.

Relate stiffness to the purpose of the calculation

Stiffness depends on the problem, tolerance, and method. Even with A- or L-stability, examine nonlinear-solve error, temporal accuracy, positivity, and wave phase separately. Removing a stiff decaying transient and preserving the geometry of an oscillation are different goals.

A small worked example

With λ=-40 and Δt=0.25, z=-10. The exact one-step multiplier is exp(-10), compared with 1/11 for backward Euler and -2/3 for the trapezoidal rule.

Both numerical multipliers have magnitude at most 1. Yet the trapezoidal rule produces artificial sign alternation. Read this as a stiff-transient damping and accuracy issue, rather than unbounded instability.

from math import exp
z = -10.0
print("exact", exp(z))
print("backward", 1/(1-z))
print("trapezoidal", (1+z/2)/(1-z/2))

The figure compares backward Euler, the trapezoidal rule, and the exact solution. Forward Euler amplifies at this step and is recorded as a separate diagnostic. Visible trapezoidal oscillation and unbounded growth are different observations.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: Does L-stability imply A-stability?

Show answer

Yes, under this definition. L-stability adds a strong-damping condition to A-stability.

Question 2: Does a negative trapezoidal value in this example violate A-stability?

Show answer

No. The sign can alternate while |R|≤1. Damping of stiff components and positivity are separate requirements.

Common pitfalls

  • Do not interpret A-stability as accuracy for any step size.
  • Do not confuse stiff-decay arguments with preservation of Hamiltonian structure.

Key points

  • A-stability covers the whole left half-plane.
  • L-stability also asks how stiff components disappear.
