Turning boundary conditions into discrete equations

Learning goal: Use boundary nodes, half cells, ghost points, and normal directions to identify errors caused by low-order boundary closures.

Second-order interior differences do not prevent an inaccurate boundary approximation from dominating the total error. Whether unknowns lie at boundary nodes or cell centers changes the distance from the boundary data to the first unknown.

This chapter uses a nodal grid including both endpoints. Do not paste half-cell or ghost-point formulas for a cell-centered grid directly onto this grid.

The half control volume at an endpoint

Integrate-u″=f over the left half cell and approximate the derivative by a difference between neighboring nodes. This gives the relation below involving the left outward normal derivative g_L. The right-hand-side contribution from f also has half weight at the endpoint.

An omitted term can affect the whole solution

Dropping h²f₀/2 leaves a first-order error in the boundary-derivative approximation. The resulting solution can have first-order error even with second-order interior equations. The manufactured solution exp(x) demonstrates this difference.

Robin conditions retain an unknown boundary value

Write the Robin data as g_R and substitute ∂_n u=(g_R-αu)/β. This adds αh/β to the endpoint diagonal coefficient. Here α>0 and β>0 are fixed, and interior and boundary equations are solved together. Nonuniform grids or curved boundaries require a new derivation.

A small worked example

Take u=exp(x), f=-exp(x), left Neumann data, and right Dirichlet data on N=8,16,32,64 intervals. Both methods use the same interior equation.

Compare mixed-good, which retains the half-cell f term, with mixed-naive, which omits it. A small residual of the implemented boundary equation alone does not guarantee accuracy relative to the continuous boundary condition.

from lab import poisson
for kind in ["mixed-good", "mixed-naive"]:
    x, u, ref, residual = poisson(64, kind)
    error = max(abs(a-b) for a,b in zip(u,ref))
    print(kind, error, residual)

In this fixed example, the appropriate closure is approximately second order and the omitted-term closure approximately first order. This is a measurement for a selected smooth one-dimensional example, not a guarantee for every boundary geometry.

Review questions and answers

Question 1: If the endpoint f term is omitted, must the whole method still be second order because its interior is second order?

Show answer

No. In this manufactured example the boundary approximation dominates, giving approximately first-order convergence.

Question 2: Can nodal and cell-centered boundary formulas be reused unchanged?

Show answer

No. Distances from the boundary to unknowns and quadrature weights differ.

Common pitfalls

  • Keep the endpoint half weights.
  • Distinguish solving a discrete boundary equation from achieving boundary accuracy for the PDE.

Key points

  • Derive interior and boundary equations together.
  • Keep the interior scheme fixed when measuring boundary-treatment differences.
