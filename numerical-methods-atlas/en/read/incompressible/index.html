Incompressible flow: project with compatible discrete operators

Implemented: unit-density periodic 2D Navier–Stokes on a MAC grid, centered convection and viscosity, projected SSPRK2, and discrete-FFT Poisson inversion.

1968: the projection branch#

Chorin (1968) is the original projection reference. Our periodic RK model illustrates the principle; it does not reproduce his boundary treatments or original time step.

Velocity components live on different faces; pressure lives at cell centers. Choose divergence D and gradient G so that D=−Gᵀ under the periodic grid inner product. Then

DGϕ=Du∗,u=u∗−Gϕ,Du=0. DG\phi=D\boldsymbol u^*,\qquad \boldsymbol u=\boldsymbol u^*-G\phi,\qquad D\boldsymbol u=0. DGϕ=Du∗,u=u∗−Gϕ,Du=0.

The FFT must invert the symbol of DG, not continuum −|k|²:

λh=−4h2[sin⁡2(kxh/2)+sin⁡2(kyh/2)]. \lambda_h=-\frac4{h^2} \left[\sin^2(k_xh/2)+\sin^2(k_yh/2)\right]. λh​=−h24​[sin2(kx​h/2)+sin2(ky​h/2)].

The pressure constant is undetermined. Set its mean to zero; subtract the mean from the Poisson source for periodic compatibility. Tests check the adjoint identity, projection idempotence, energy contraction and divergence removal.

Taylor–Green verification#

On [0,2π)² with viscosity ν, use

u=sin⁡xcos⁡y e−2νt,v=−cos⁡xsin⁡y e−2νt. u=\sin x\cos y\,e^{-2\nu t},\qquad v=-\cos x\sin y\,e^{-2\nu t}. u=sinxcosye−2νt,v=−cosxsinye−2νt.

The velocity error refines at second order for N=16,32,64 at T=0.5, ν=0.1. Mean kinetic energy is e^(−4νt)/4. Stage projection multipliers are not silently labeled physical pressure; a final pressure estimate is reconstructed from the momentum RHS, without a claimed pressure-order verification.

Reproducible measurements include maximum absolute discrete divergence. The explicit step retains both advective and viscous restrictions. No-slip cavity boundaries, SIMPLE/PISO, cylinder wakes, mixed FEM, turbulence models and experimental validation are not implemented by this periodic test. Compare the separate low-Mach LBM model.

The Arakawa A/B/C comparison places this MAC arrangement in a geophysical context. Matching face locations does not automatically supply a Coriolis or potential-vorticity algorithm.
