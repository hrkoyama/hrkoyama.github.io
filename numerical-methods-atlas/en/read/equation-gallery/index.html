Equation gallery: choose the equation before the algorithm

The local Web edition provides an Equations gallery with saved profiles, physical-time controls, fixed plot ranges, initial/boundary conditions, characteristic speeds and links back to methods and papers. It contains nine cases across seven equation families. Data are generated by python scripts/build_equation_gallery.py, not invented animation curves.

Translation and diffusion#

Linear advection u_t+u_x=0 moves a periodic sine wave. First-order upwind with forward Euler is compared with exact translated cell averages. Heat diffusion u_t=0.02u_xx damps the same Fourier mode; centered spatial differences and forward Euler use νΔt/Δx²≤0.4. Their discrete means are conserved.

Burgers: shocks and fans#

Burgers (1948) is the classical model reference; this exhibit uses its inviscid scalar conservation law:

ut+(u2/2)x=0,f′(u)=u. u_t+(u^2/2)_x=0,\qquad f'(u)=u. ut​+(u2/2)x​=0,f′(u)=u.

A decreasing jump forms a shock traveling at (u_L+u_R)/2. An increasing jump forms a rarefaction fan, with u=x/t in coordinates centered on the initial jump. Exact Godunov flux selects the entropy solution, including zero flux inside a transonic Burgers fan. The plotted exact curves are cell integrals, so a shock cutting a cell is not treated as a point sample.

Traffic: a jam can move against the cars#

Lighthill–Whitham (1955) and Richards (1956) are the independent classical traffic references. This exhibit chooses a normalized quadratic fundamental diagram, rather than asserting that every traffic model has it:

ρt+[ρ(1−ρ)]x=0,0≤ρ≤1. \rho_t+[\rho(1-\rho)]_x=0,\qquad 0\leq\rho\leq1. ρt​+[ρ(1−ρ)]x​=0,0≤ρ≤1.

Vehicles move at 1−ρ, while small density disturbances move at 1−2ρ. A jump from 0.3 to 0.9 shocks at speed −0.2, despite positive vehicle speeds. The opposite ordering creates a clearing fan because the flux is concave. Both Burgers and traffic use MUSCL minmod, Godunov flux, SSPRK3 and CFL≤0.4. Density bounds are checked; the solver does not silently clip them.

Fluid systems and kinetic methods#

The existing Euler Sod, wet shallow-water dam-break and isothermal density-jump solvers are connected as additional cases. All three now overlay conserved cell means from semi-analytic Riemann references: known wave fronts split Gauss quadrature, and the 24/48-point difference estimates integration convergence. The reference library explains the formulas, root residuals and point-versus-mean convention. Dry-bed reference formulas do not extend the positive-state numerical solver to dry states. Those models and positive-depth limits are defined in the benchmark guide.

LBM, Chapman–Enskog expansion, periodic incompressible flow, and hyperbolic diffusion have separate computed exhibits. An equation, a kinetic model and a discretization are different choices.

Saved times and reproducibility#

Scalar snapshots observe completed solver steps: their actual times can differ slightly from the requested display times. Output requests leave the trajectory unchanged. The existing 1D system baseline has no snapshot API; its displayed fluid times are explicitly labeled independent solves, each ending at the shown time. Playback links those runs and does not imply identical intermediate steps. Outflow mean changes include boundary flux and are not labeled mass loss.

Download profiles and source hashes. Additional convergence evidence keeps these new experiments separate from the atlas's original validation manifest.

Additional equation maps#

The biological survey catalogs 22 model families with original references and proposed verification, without adding biological simulations. The Arakawa grid chapter adds computed A/B/C Fourier-symbol comparisons for linear rotating shallow water, rather than another fluid movie. These statuses remain distinct from the nine precomputed cases above.

For definitions and comparisons of A-stability, L-stability and L1/L2/L∞ error norms, see the shared accuracy and stability guide.
