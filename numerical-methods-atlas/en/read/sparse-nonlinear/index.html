Sparse and nonlinear systems: measure the actual residual

Implemented: COO-to-CSR assembly, restarted GMRES, BiCGSTAB, Jacobi and unpivoted ILU(0), and Armijo-damped Newton with dense or Krylov inner solves. These are small, inspectable real-arithmetic examples.

1986–1992: nonsymmetric Krylov methods#

Saad–Schultz (1986) introduces GMRES; van der Vorst (1992) introduces BiCGSTAB. The implemented GMRES uses Arnoldi reorthogonalization and small Hessenberg least squares, restarting after a specified number of inner iterations. BiCGSTAB has explicit breakdown reporting; it need not have monotone residuals.

Both implementations apply a fixed right preconditioner:

AM−1z=b,x=M−1z,∥b−Ax∥2≤max⁡(atol,rtol∥b∥2). A M^{-1} z=b,\qquad x=M^{-1}z, \qquad \|b-Ax\|_2\leq\max(\mathrm{atol},\mathrm{rtol}\|b\|_2). AM−1z=b,x=M−1z,∥b−Ax∥2​≤max(atol,rtol∥b∥2​).

The stopping history recomputes the true residual. Jacobi uses diagonal scaling. ILU(0) retains the supplied nonzero structure, with no fill, pivoting or reordering; tiny pivots raise an explicit failure. This is not a robust replacement for a production sparse factorization.

Newton and conditioning#

Newton solves J(x)δ=−F(x), then chooses a backtracked step length with an Armijo residual decrease. The Newton–Krylov demonstration assembles J and uses GMRES; it is not matrix free. Bratu's equation is checked against an analytic solution at two spatial resolutions, including inner-solve failures.

A small residual is not automatically a small solution error:

∥x−x^∥∥x∥≤κ(A)∥b−Ax^∥∥b∥. \frac{\|x-\hat x\|}{\|x\|} \leq \kappa(A)\frac{\|b-A\hat x\|}{\|b\|}. ∥x∥∥x−x^∥​≤κ(A)∥b∥∥b−Ax^∥​.

Condition number, eigenvalue distribution and nonnormality describe different features. A smaller condition number alone does not rank restarted GMRES. CG still requires an SPD operator and suitable SPD preconditioning; generic ILU(0) is not asserted to satisfy that requirement.

The nonsymmetric example is first-order upwind advection plus centered diffusion with zero Dirichlet data. Iteration counts have different per-iteration costs; the figure makes no wall-clock ranking. See the data and source hashes, FEM assembly, and implicit ODE stages.

Shared foundation: automatic differentiation and Bratu verification

Compare analytic, finite-difference and forward-mode AD with the same residual, initial state and Newton stopping controls. Derivative error, nonlinear residual and spatial discretization error are reported separately, with dedicated code, eight tests and reproduction commands. A separate Bratu parameter-sensitivity chapter verifies implicit differentiation and discrete adjoints. A reverse-mode AD engine remains unimplemented.
