Implementation roadmap

Editorial scope

The focus is mathematical methods, formulations, applicability and verification. AMR (adaptive mesh refinement), parallelization and mixed-precision techniques for computational efficiency are outside the scope of dedicated coverage and development. Ordinary roundoff and numerical stability remain foundational topics.

Current development plan and published examples

Build a development foundation that lets a user’s AI agent obtain implementations, assumptions, benchmark problems and references from the Atlas, then assemble, modify, run and verify code for the requested calculation. Demonstrate this complete development cycle on a few existing-field tasks by year end. Public reproduction, maintenance and restoration support that goal. The 16 December retreat is the first presentation target: an A0 portrait poster, mainly Japanese with English technical terms, reporting the demonstrated results.

The central year-end deliverable is a prototype in which a user’s AI agent uses public resources and machine-readable manifests to start from Atlas verified implementations, develop the requested code, assess it with Atlas benchmarks and return it to the user. Start with bounded 1D advection and diffusion. Provide bilingual requirements, applicability, retrieval, dependencies, execution, output contracts, references, criteria, failure reasons and citation. Development history and field-specific validation candidates

The sections below retain earlier development records and field-specific validation candidates. They are not the current priority order or a commitment to implement every item this year. Existing section links are preserved.

Earlier priority: shock methods#

At that stage, shock-method expansion took priority over relativity and new subject areas. The first published stage adds limited parabolic FV, characteristics, WENO-Z and an AUSM baseline, Roe's Property U with numerical counterexamples, and controlled planar odd–even diagnostics. The second computed stage adds direct-Eulerian traced PPM with independent steepening/flattening switches, specified AUSM+/AUSM+-up fluxes, and conforming-grid cylinder-forebody tests including a directional HLLE/Roe experiment. The recorded strong-jump AUSM+ failure, finite-time bow-shock behavior and unresolved verification are explicit. Lagrange/remap, optimized original Rotated-RHLL, broader strength/rotation studies and external-code comparisons remain future work.

First edition: executable foundations#

Version 0.1.0 brings together an educational Python library, a historical atlas, English method notes, a bibliographic catalog, comparison figures, and automated numerical checks. The repository is the first-edition distribution; the reading guide in the README connects the material.

The NASA case catalog includes computed 15-degree wedge baselines and remaining implementation candidates. A separate conforming-grid forebody diagnostic now includes a directional hybrid; catalog inclusion alone still does not indicate an implemented simulation.

Earlier validation candidates#

  • Strengthen links between run manifests and saved arrays; report grid and time refinement together with wall-clock costs.
  • Add finite-volume cell-average initialization and integrated references, characteristic WENO, positivity limiters, and stronger shock tests.
  • Added shallow-water and isothermal Riemann references; topography, well balancing, and numerical dry-bed evolution remain separate implementation steps.
  • Extend the new HLLD standard tests with UCT-HLLD, stronger-field cases and external reference-code comparisons.
  • Explore nonlinear Fourier methods and dealiasing, Euler DG with limiting, and multidimensional residual distribution.
  • Implement low-Mach PDE preconditioning and extend the directional 2D Euler hybrid beyond its stated forebody tests; test entropy correction, positivity, and carbuncle resistance separately.
  • Extend elliptic solvers to variable coefficients, anisotropy, line smoothing, FAS, AMG, and 2D mixed boundaries.

Earlier suggested order for NASA cases#

Start with wedge and expansion flows to verify 2D Euler fluxes and wall boundaries. Use the cavity and Blasius cases to establish viscous, pressure, and boundary treatments, then the cylinder to examine unsteady accuracy. For RANS, verify model implementations on flat-plate and bump cases before moving to airfoils, steps, diffusers, and humps. This order follows dependencies and difficulty and complements the atlas's publication chronology.

Shock gallery: NASA wedges and a forward-facing step#

Computed the NASA 15-degree wedges at Mach 2.0 and 2.5 with independent body-fitted first-order HLL / SSPRK2 finite volumes, two grids and oblique-shock references. Added the classical Mach-3 forward step on a 240 × 80 blocked-cell mesh, 81 saved states to t = 4 and an MP4, bringing the movie catalog to five. The step uses plain reflecting corners, not the entropy/enthalpy reset of the original PPM comparison. These are educational inviscid baselines, not Wind-US outputs or viscous experimental validations. Setup and limits. Mach-5 shock/boundary-layer interaction, Mach-7 viscous ramps and the existing transonic diffuser/airfoil cases remain implementation candidates.

Web edition#

The public web edition organizes the first-edition library as a historical atlas, method reading room, computed gallery, and searchable reference library. Each exhibit connects literature, equations, execution, figures, and verification scope.

The interactive Sod comparison switches between four precomputed MUSCL density curves from the Python library. Figures and the original gallery validation manifest remain available alongside the complete source edition. Future exhibits should continue to distinguish tested educational examples, original-figure reproductions, prototypes, literature-only entries, and planned work.

First expansion: interactive numerical notebooks#

The stability laboratory runs periodic FTCS, upwind, classical LF, and LW updates in the browser, with both velocity signs, Fourier amplification, pointwise error, and downloadable experiment snapshots. Its numerical kernels are checked against the Python implementation across 24 reference cases.

The convergence notebook makes the existing DG and Euler entropy-wave gallery measurements inspectable, including observed slopes and scalar-unknown counts. It introduces no new wall-clock ranking or general nonlinear stability claim. The experiment guide records definitions and provenance.

Second expansion: geometry, time, and multidimensional shocks#

Added seven scalar ODE integrators with a temporal notebook; three mesh-connectivity examples with an explorer; 1D SPH kernel reconstruction; a 1D ALE geometric-conservation check; and cylindrical gradient, Newtonian stress, and stress-divergence operators verified against analytic fields. The coordinate and viscosity chapter distinguishes physical components from coordinate-basis tensors.

The 2D Double Mach Reflection baseline now computes an actual Mach-10 interaction with first-order HLL and SSPRK2. Higher-order reconstruction, full grid refinement, and resolution of the fine wall jet remain future work. The NASA wedge baselines were added later; the cataloged viscous and turbulence cases remain unimplemented. Blunt-body carbuncle comparisons beyond the new planar diagnostic, a complete SPH fluid solver, unstructured flow fluxes, and a complete ALE Euler solver also remain future work. The Roe memorial is sourced from official university records.

Third expansion: Hamiltonian maps and particle gravity#

Added fixed-step canonical maps and a notebook for harmonic/quartic oscillators; periodic 3D CIC/FFT PM; an isolated softened octree with monopole/full second moments; isolated doubled-grid convolution; and sampled/continuum slab Green-transform options. Figures have dedicated source manifests. P³M corrections, a coupled TreePM solver, FMM translations, cosmological expansion, shear-periodic remapping, and a gravity-coupled SPH fluid integrator remain future work.

Fourth expansion: nonsteady movies and smooth KEEP#

Added saved-state output that leaves solver time steps unchanged, four numerical movies with frame inspection and MP4 export, and the original second-order Cartesian KEEP flux with smooth-vortex refinement. Steady fields retain still images. The KEEP implementation excludes shock stabilization, later PEP/redefined variants, high-order curvilinear forms, and the 3D TGV/LES examples discussed in the reading guide.

Fifth expansion: limiters, RK families, and CIP#

Added selectable MUSCL slopes, TVB modified-minmod and positive-reconstruction building blocks, nine explicit RK tableaus with nonlinear stage verification, twelve oscillator methods, and original cubic-Hermite CIP with a conservation counterexample. Full Euler-update positivity is still a separate extension.

Sixth expansion: equations, sparse systems and kinetic transport#

Added nine saved-profile cases across seven equation families, including Burgers and LWR traffic. Scalar exact cell averages and conserved cell means of semi-analytic Euler/shallow-water/isothermal Riemann references are distinguished. The reference library adds 18 formula-based cases, including vacuum/dry fans, viscous profiles and a manufactured Poisson field.

The new continuous P1 FEM, sparse/nonlinear solvers, stiff ODE integrators, periodic MAC projection, D2Q9–BGK, and first-order hyperbolic diffusion baseline have dedicated numerical checks, figures and source-hashed manifests. Chapman–Enskog expansion is a formal derivation linked to the measured shear viscosity. These educational additions are included in the published atlas at https://hrkoyama.github.io/numerical-methods-atlas/ . Their implementation limits and remaining verification work are stated in each chapter.

Earlier implementation candidates and progress#

  • Added — Roe's Property U: original four conditions, parameter-vector derivation, jump/consistency/eigenbasis checks and an expansion-discontinuity counterexample; see the explanation.
  • Inutsuka's GSPH: Gaussian convolution and specific-volume reconstruction, oriented pair Riemann states, a matched conservative energy update, and smooth/contact/shock-tube verification before variable smoothing lengths. The Iwasaki–Inutsuka GSPMHD extension is a later, separately verified stage; see the source notes and staged plan.
  • Relativistic hydrodynamics is a source-based extension plan: SRHD recovery and characteristic tests before SRMHD, fixed-metric GR, and independently verified Einstein–matter coupling. Radiation and SRGSPH are separate branches; see the development review checked in November 2026.
  • First stage added: limited parabolic FV, conservative characteristics and FD WENO-Z with Sod/contact/Shu–Osher comparisons. Direct-Eulerian tracing/steepening/flattening are now added in the second stage; Lagrange/remap remain separate.
  • AUSM common-sound-speed baseline retained; specified AUSM+ and AUSM+-up fluxes, matched traced-PPM cases and a recorded admissibility failure are added in the second stage.
  • Completed web stage: HLLD with Brio–Wu, Alfvén-wave refinement and weak field-loop CT-Contact/GLM comparisons; see the MHD tests.
  • Scoped implementation and validation — Yee FDTD: 2D TMz vacuum periodic plane waves and a rectangular PEC cavity now have original code, fixed inputs, analytic comparisons, grid/time refinement, dispersion and twelve-period discrete-energy checks. Reading, code and results. The retained 2D interface, PML and p-polarized examples are joined by normally incident 1D finite Ohmic and Lorentz slabs. Complex-permittivity slab solutions check reflection, transmission and absorption. Directly accumulated dissipation, material oscillator storage, discrete and physical energy budgets, and grid refinement are separate checks. General 2D dispersive media and dispersive PML are outside the validated scope.
  • Planned — plasma PIC (Particle-in-Cell): start with electrostatic particle–Poisson coupling, then electromagnetic particle–Maxwell coupling. Cover particle shapes, deposition/gather, Boris pushing, charge-conserving current deposition and Gauss-law diagnostics. Plan plasma-oscillation, two-stream and Landau-damping tests, with energy, noise and grid/time/particle refinement checks. Existing gravitational PM is a separate scoped example. The WarpX PIC theory notes are a starting reference; no plasma PIC solver is implemented here yet.
  • Stiff integration: higher-order IMEX, embedded DIRK, variable-step BDF and operator splitting beyond the implemented BE/BDF2/SDIRK2/IMEX-Euler models.
  • Sparse/nonlinear solvers: matrix-free Newton–Krylov, robust reorderings/fill, flexible preconditioning and broader nonlinear benchmarks beyond the implemented fixed-right GMRES/BiCGSTAB and ILU(0).
  • Viscous/incompressible flow: wall projection, SIMPLE/PISO, cavity and cylinder-wake validation beyond periodic Taylor–Green.
  • FEM: mixed velocity–pressure pairs, inf–sup checks, curved/irregular meshes and natural/mixed boundary assembly.
  • LBM: MRT/TRT/cumulant collisions, thermal and multiphase models beyond low-Mach isothermal D2Q9–BGK.
  • Hyperbolic diffusion: full second-order RD, anisotropy and unified advection/viscous systems beyond the periodic first-order baseline.

AMR is outside the editorial scope.

Biological and geophysical survey additions#

The biological catalog contains 22 survey-only model families, with equations, source conventions and proposed verification. A practical implementation sequence is logistic/SIR and enzyme kinetics, then Fisher fronts, FitzHugh–Nagumo, Schnakenberg and Keller–Segel, with a birth–death SSA as a separate stochastic branch. No biological solver or biological validation is added by the survey.

Arakawa A/B/C has variable-location diagrams and tested semi-discrete f-plane Fourier symbols. D/E, vertical staggering, nonlinear potential-vorticity schemes, Kelvin/Rossby waves and coastlines remain additional work. This is not a full geophysical dynamical core.

Longer-term candidates: machine-learning numerics#

Status: revision candidates; the dedicated explanations, implementations and verification below have not been added. Start with stable function evaluation, automatic differentiation and optimization as shared foundations for Volume 1. Scientific machine-learning applications are later candidates. These plans do not add to implemented-method counts, and no release date is assigned.

Mixed-precision arithmetic and the design of low-precision GPU computations are outside the current scope. Ordinary floating-point roundoff and stable function evaluation remain part of the shared foundations.

  • Softmax and log-sum-exp: maximum shifting, log-softmax, exponential overflow and underflow. Plan checks for large inputs, invariance under constant shifts, probability sums and derivative consistency. See the SciPy softmax notes.
  • Cross-entropy and stable loss evaluation: compute directly from logits, connect to log-sum-exp, and cover sigmoid and binary cross-entropy. Check finite values and analytic gradients for extreme inputs. Start from the PyTorch cross-entropy documentation.
  • Automatic differentiation and backpropagation: forward/reverse modes, the chain rule, Jacobian–vector products (JVPs), vector–Jacobian products (VJPs) and Hessian–vector products. Distinguish symbolic and finite-difference derivatives; cover roundoff, nondifferentiable points and intermediate-storage costs. Use the JAX autodiff guide and plan analytic/directional derivative comparisons.
  • Implicit differentiation, adjoints and differentiable solvers: sensitivities through linear solves, converged nonlinear systems and time evolution. Distinguish unrolling iterations from differentiating the defining equations; state Jacobian invertibility, solve tolerances and gradient checks. The implicit-differentiation paper is a starting reference.
  • Machine-learning optimization: gradient descent/SGD, momentum, Adam, L-BFGS and Gauss–Newton/Levenberg–Marquardt. Distinguish objective minimization from the existing Newton root solves; compare scaling, conditioning, learning rates and gradient norms. Do not claim universal convergence or a universal ranking.
  • Application candidate — PINNs and inverse problems: separate PDE, initial/boundary and observation losses; compare with conventional finite differences/FEM using shared error measures. A lower training loss alone is not a PDE-error guarantee. See the authors' PINN notes and failure-mode study.
  • Application candidate — neural operators/Fourier neural operators: connect learned function-to-solution maps with the existing Fourier bases and spectral methods. Plan separate checks of out-of-distribution behavior, grid changes, discretization and aliasing errors; changing grid resolution alone does not guarantee accuracy. See the neural-operator paper.
  • Application candidate — symbolic regression and equation discovery: search for explicit expressions from data or learned models, separately from automatic derivative evaluation. Assess noise, dimensions/symmetries, expression complexity and independent-data checks. Consider the study on extracting symbolic models from deep learning.
