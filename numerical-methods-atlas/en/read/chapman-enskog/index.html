Chapman–Enskog expansion: from populations to transport

Chapman–Enskog（チャップマン–エンスコッグ）expansion separates kinetic relaxation from slowly varying hydrodynamic fields. The classical kinetic theory is treated by Chapman and Cowling's The Mathematical Theory of Non-Uniform Gases (first edition 1939; third edition 1970). The book's date is not the invention date of the expansion. This chapter gives an independent, unforced, isothermal lattice-BGK derivation at low Mach number.

A small parameter and solvability#

Introduce a bookkeeping parameter ε for slow variation:

fq=fq(0)+ϵfq(1)+ϵ2fq(2)+⋯ ,∂t=ϵ∂t1+ϵ2∂t2,∇=ϵ∇1. f_q=f_q^{(0)}+\epsilon f_q^{(1)}+\epsilon^2 f_q^{(2)}+\cdots, \quad \partial_t=\epsilon\partial_{t_1}+\epsilon^2\partial_{t_2}, \quad \nabla=\epsilon\nabla_1. fq​=fq(0)​+ϵfq(1)​+ϵ2fq(2)​+⋯,∂t​=ϵ∂t1​​+ϵ2∂t2​​,∇=ϵ∇1​.

Conserved moments are carried by the local equilibrium, so higher-order corrections have zero mass and momentum moments:

∑qfq(k)=0,∑qcqfq(k)=0(k≥1). \sum_q f_q^{(k)}=0,\quad \sum_q\boldsymbol c_q f_q^{(k)}=0\quad (k\geq1). q∑​fq(k)​=0,q∑​cq​fq(k)​=0(k≥1).

These are constraints on the expansion, not instructions to erase the nonequilibrium stress. The stress moment of f^(1) produces viscous transport.

Keep the discrete-time Taylor term#

For lattice BGK, expand the streaming side with D_q=∂t+c_q·∇. In lattice time units the Taylor series starts as D_q f_q + D_q² f_q/2. At successive orders,

fq(0)=fqeq,fq(1)=−τD1qfqeq, f_q^{(0)}=f_q^{eq},\qquad f_q^{(1)}=-\tau D_{1q} f_q^{eq}, fq(0)​=fqeq​,fq(1)​=−τD1q​fqeq​,
∂t2fqeq+(1−12τ)D1qfq(1)=−fq(2)τ. \partial_{t_2} f_q^{eq} +\left(1-\frac1{2\tau}\right)D_{1q}f_q^{(1)} =-\frac{f_q^{(2)}}{\tau}. ∂t2​​fqeq​+(1−2τ1​)D1q​fq(1)​=−τfq(2)​​.

The first-order mass and momentum moments give the continuity and Euler parts. The second-order stress correction supplies the Newtonian viscous part. D2Q9 equilibrium isotropy, the low-Mach approximation, and the preceding factor give the shear viscosity

ν=cs2(τ−12)Δt. \nu=c_s^2\left(\tau-\frac12\right)\Delta t. ν=cs2​(τ−21​)Δt.

Here τ is the dimensionless number of lattice time steps used for relaxation, with physical τ_relax=τΔt. Continuous-time BGK instead gives ν=c_s²τ_relax for the corresponding isothermal model; there is no streaming Taylor half-step in that continuous equation. Mixing those definitions yields a wrong viscosity.

What this derivation does and does not establish#

The D2Q9 experiment measures shear damping rather than only substituting this formula. Qian et al. (1992) is the lattice-model source; the BGK source concerns continuous kinetic relaxation. Guo et al. (2002) treats the force discretization separately; the unforced algebra above does not derive its source term.

This is a formal near-equilibrium asymptotic expansion, with small gradients and small Mach number for the truncated isothermal equilibrium. It does not prove stability near shocks, justify arbitrary relaxation times, recover an energy equation, or make this model a general rarefied-gas Boltzmann solver. Burnett-order transport and thermal collision models require additional moments and analysis.
