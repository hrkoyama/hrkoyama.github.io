Advanced limiters: TVB and positivity preservation

The limiter notebook includes a TVB scalar experiment and a separate conservative Euler-state scaling demonstration. These address different failure modes: loss of accuracy near a smooth extremum and inadmissible thermodynamic states.

TVB modified minmod#

For a candidate increment v and neighboring increments a and b, define

m~(v,a,b)={v,∣v∣≤MΔx2,m(v,a,b),otherwise, \widetilde m(v,a,b)= \begin{cases} v,&|v|\le M\Delta x^2,\\ m(v,a,b),&\text{otherwise}, \end{cases} m(v,a,b)={v,m(v,a,b),​∣v∣≤MΔx2,otherwise,​

where m is the generalized minmod: the smallest magnitude with a shared sign, and zero when signs disagree. Our scalar demonstration chooses v=(a+b)/2. At M=0 this reduces to the two-increment minmod used in the TVD experiment. At M>0 sufficiently small increments survive even when the neighboring slopes have opposite signs, permitting a smooth peak to remain curved.

The dx-squared threshold reflects the scale of smooth-extremum face deviations in the RKDG setting. Its coefficient has units of solution divided by length squared; it is not a universal dimensionless constant. Rescaling the dependent variable or coordinates requires reconsidering M. Increments and derivatives require different thresholds.

Cockburn and Shu (1989) develop the TVB RKDG framework. This edition implements the modified-minmod building block in scalar finite volumes, rather than that full RKDG method or its theorem. The notebook computes M=0,10,50 on both the sine and square pulse. Compare errors and measured maximum TV: allowing small extrema means TV need not decrease at every step, and a large M can also admit small oscillations beside a discontinuity. Changing M does not prove a uniform TVB bound for an arbitrary method. No Euler-system TVB extension is implemented.

Positive density and pressure are different constraints#

For ideal-gas conservative variables U=(rho,m,E),

p(U)=(γ−1)(E−∣m∣22ρ),G={U:ρ>0, p(U)>0}. p(U)=(\gamma-1)\left(E-\frac{|m|^2}{2\rho}\right),\qquad \mathcal G=\{U:\rho>0,\ p(U)>0\}. p(U)=(γ−1)(E−2ρ∣m∣2​),G={U:ρ>0, p(U)>0}.

Positive density alone does not ensure positive internal energy. TVD, TVB, an entropy fix, and the absence of visible wiggles do not automatically make Euler states admissible. A negative pressure can arise from excessive kinetic energy even when rho and E are positive.

A mean-preserving scaling building block#

Start with an admissible conservative cell average Ubar. Scale every supplied reconstruction point by the same factor:

U^α=Uˉ+θ(Uα−Uˉ),0≤θ≤1. \widehat U_\alpha=\bar U+\theta(U_\alpha-\bar U),\qquad 0\le\theta\le1. Uα​=Uˉ+θ(Uα​−Uˉ),0≤θ≤1.

If the original points have quadrature mean Ubar, the scaled points retain it. Using a single theta across components and points is essential to this particular construction. Clipping rho, pressure, or total energy independently does not have that conservation property.

First bound theta by the minimum reconstructed density:

θρ=min⁡(1,ρˉ−ϵρρˉ−ρmin⁡)if ρmin⁡<ϵρ;θρ=1 otherwise. \theta_\rho=\min\left(1, \frac{\bar\rho-\epsilon_\rho}{\bar\rho-\rho_{\min}}\right) \quad\text{if }\rho_{\min}<\epsilon_\rho; \qquad\theta_\rho=1\text{ otherwise}. θρ​=min(1,ρˉ​−ρmin​ρˉ​−ϵρ​​)if ρmin​<ϵρ​;θρ​=1 otherwise.

Then shrink theta in [0,theta_rho] until every supplied point also satisfies p(Uhat) ≥ epsilon_p. Ideal-gas pressure is concave for positive density, so the admissible part of this line segment contains the mean and can be found by bisection. The implementation uses 64 iterations and a small roundoff retreat. Density and pressure floors are input parameters, not inserted mass or energy. Inadmissible averages raise an error; scaling around a bad mean cannot repair conservation and positivity together in this way.

This follows the convex-scaling idea of Zhang and Shu (2010), with a deliberately simple common-state scaling and numerical pressure search. It is not a line-by-line reproduction of their two-stage density-polynomial and state limiter. The implementation covers the supplied points only; arbitrary polynomial values between those points are not automatically bounded.

What the demonstration actually verifies#

The three-node cell has xi=-1,0,1, positive Gauss–Lobatto weights (1/6,2/3,1/6), gamma=1.4, and Ubar=(1,0,2.5). The raw states are Ubar + xi times delta. Choose delta=(1.2,0,0) for a density violation, (0,3,0) for a pressure violation, or (1.2,3,1) for a combined violation. Their weighted mean is exactly Ubar in real arithmetic. The plot samples only these three nodes and reports theta, minimum limited density, minimum limited pressure, and the conservative mean defect, with epsilon_rho=epsilon_p=1e-8. These are constructed reconstruction tests, not blast-wave or rarefaction time integrations.

import numpy as np
from numart.limiters import positive_reconstruction

mean = np.array([1., 0., 2.5])
raw = mean + np.array([-1., 0., 1.])[:, None] * [0., 3., 0.]
limited, theta = positive_reconstruction(mean, raw)

Tests check that an already admissible reconstruction is unchanged, bad means are rejected, and both floors and conservative quadrature means survive the three failure cases.

From reconstruction to a fully discrete guarantee#

A complete positivity-preserving Euler method also needs a compatible positivity-preserving numerical flux, positive quadrature decomposition including interface states, a corresponding wave-speed/CFL restriction, and limiting at all required stages. SSP time stepping propagates a forward-Euler positivity property only when that property and its step restriction already hold. Source terms and boundary treatment require their own checks.

In the original Numart release, this helper is not wired into the existing Euler MUSCL, finite-difference WENO, CT/GLM, or KEEP time updates. That immutable release and the existing solvers retain their documented limitations. A separately distributed example now checks one-dimensional Euler MUSCL stage updates. Finite-difference WENO, CT/GLM, KEEP, strong blasts, multidimensional flow and non-ideal equations of state remain outside this verification.
