Lattice Boltzmann: collide, stream, recover fluid motion

Implemented: low-Mach, isothermal D2Q9–BGK, Guo body forcing, periodic boundaries and stationary half-way bounce-back walls. Lattice units use Δx=Δt=ρ_ref=1, c_s²=1/3.

1954 → 1992 → 2002#

The relaxation model of Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (1954) precedes lattice BGK. Qian–d'Humières–Lallemand (1992) is the lattice-BGK reference. Guo–Zheng–Shi (2002) supplies the force discretization used here. These are distinct developments.

D2Q9 has one resting population, four axial directions and four diagonals. Weights are 4/9, 1/9 and 1/36 for those groups. The equilibrium is

fqeq=wqρ[1+3cq⋅u+92(cq⋅u)2−32∣u∣2]. f_q^{eq}=w_q\rho\left[1+3\boldsymbol c_q\cdot\boldsymbol u +\frac92(\boldsymbol c_q\cdot\boldsymbol u)^2 -\frac32|\boldsymbol u|^2\right]. fqeq​=wq​ρ[1+3cq​⋅u+29​(cq​⋅u)2−23​∣u∣2].

Collision relaxes populations, then streaming shifts each by its integer lattice velocity:

fq(x+cq,t+1)=fq(x,t)−fq−fqeqτ+Sq. f_q(\boldsymbol x+\boldsymbol c_q,t+1) =f_q(\boldsymbol x,t)-\frac{f_q-f_q^{eq}}{\tau}+S_q. fq​(x+cq​,t+1)=fq​(x,t)−τfq​−fqeq​​+Sq​.

For a force density F, Guo forcing uses

Sq=wq(1−12τ)[3(cq−u)+9(cq⋅u)cq]⋅F,ρu=∑qfqcq+F2. S_q=w_q\left(1-\frac1{2\tau}\right) \left[3(\boldsymbol c_q-\boldsymbol u) +9(\boldsymbol c_q\cdot\boldsymbol u)\boldsymbol c_q\right]\cdot\boldsymbol F, \quad \rho\boldsymbol u=\sum_q f_q\boldsymbol c_q+\frac{\boldsymbol F}{2}. Sq​=wq​(1−2τ1​)[3(cq​−u)+9(cq​⋅u)cq​]⋅F,ρu=q∑​fq​cq​+2F​.

Density is the population sum, pressure is c_s²ρ, and viscosity is ν=c_s²(τ−1/2). The Chapman–Enskog derivation explains the discrete-time half-step correction. τ>1/2 makes this viscosity positive; it is not a universal nonlinear stability certificate.

Two independent tests#

A transverse periodic shear wave decays as exp(−νk²t), allowing an independent viscosity measurement. Force-driven Poiseuille flow has exact profile u_x=F_x y(H−y)/(2ρ_refν). The walls sit half a lattice spacing beyond the outer fluid nodes, not at those node centers. Fixed τ=0.8, peak velocity 0.01, and Ny=8,16,32 test wall placement and refinement. Startup runs for twelve slowest viscous decay times; finite-time and spatial errors remain distinct.

The data manifest records channel errors, Mach numbers, force settings and step counts. Unit tests also verify periodic mass/momentum conservation and equilibrium moments. BGK is a single-relaxation baseline: MRT/TRT, entropic and cumulant collisions, thermal/compressible LBM, moving walls and multiphase models remain extensions. Projection flow solves a different discrete system and provides another route to incompressibility.
