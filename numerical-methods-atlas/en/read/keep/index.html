KEEP schemes: preserve the smooth-flow balance, test the limits

KEEP stands for kinetic-energy and entropy preserving. The work of Yuichi Kuya, Kosuke Totani, and Soshi Kawai at Tohoku University seeks a low-dissipation balance between the conservative equations of compressible flow. The original 2018 paper is the starting point. The authors’ 2022 conference explanation, especially §2.2 and Eq. (9), states the Cartesian flux implemented here.

This exhibit implements the original second-order arithmetic-mean Cartesian flux, with periodic boundaries and classical RK4. It does not implement every later scheme named KEEP. Watch its smooth-vortex evolution.

Which quantities should be preserved?#

In a smooth periodic inviscid flow, mass, momentum, and total energy are conservative. The total kinetic energy alone is generally not constant in compressible flow: pressure work exchanges kinetic and internal energy. With K = integral rho |u|²/2,

dKdt=∫p ∇⋅u dV. \frac{dK}{dt}=\int p\,\nabla\cdot u\,dV. dtdK​=∫p∇⋅udV.

A kinetic-energy-consistent convective discretization should not create a spurious kinetic-energy source through an inconsistent mass/momentum product rule. This is different from imposing dK/dt = 0 on a compressible vortex.

We measure the dimensionless entropy proxy

S∗=∫ρlog⁡(pργ)dV. S_* = \int\rho\log\left(\frac{p}{\rho^\gamma}\right)dV. S∗​=∫ρlog(ργp​)dV.

For a fixed ideal-gas composition this differs from physical entropy only by fixed factors and an additive mass term. Its change per initial mass is useful when initial isentropic data give S_* approximately zero; dividing by initial S_* would be ill-conditioned.

The semidiscrete update and two-point flux#

Let an overbar denote the arithmetic mean of left and right values, u_n the velocity normal to a Cartesian face, and e = p/[rho(gamma−1)]. The baseline flux is

C=ρ‾ u‾n,Fρui=C u‾i+p‾ δin,FE=C[12uL⋅uR+e‾]+12(un,LpR+un,RpL). \begin{aligned} C&=\overline\rho\,\overline u_n,\\ F_{\rho u_i}&=C\,\overline u_i+\overline p\,\delta_{in},\\ F_E&=C\left[\frac12\boldsymbol u_L\cdot\boldsymbol u_R+\overline e\right] +\frac12(u_{n,L}p_R+u_{n,R}p_L). \end{aligned} CFρui​​FE​​=ρ​un​,=Cui​+p​δin​,=C[21​uL​⋅uR​+e]+21​(un,L​pR​+un,R​pL​).​

The mass flux is C. The cross-velocity kinetic term and cross-pressure work are deliberate; replacing them by a convenient average of the full physical flux changes the discrete energy exchange. The implementation is a central two-point flux-difference method of second spatial order. It has no Riemann-solver dissipation.

Evaluate this two-point flux between neighboring grid nodes, then take the right-minus-left flux difference to update the conserved state. Written in the two-dimensional notation of this implementation, Kuya and Kawai (2022), §2.2, Eqs. (1), (8) and (9) give the following semidiscrete system: space is discrete while time remains continuous. E is total energy per unit volume.

qi,j=(ρ,ρu,ρv,E)i,jT,dqi,jdt=−F^i+1/2,j x−F^i−1/2,j xΔx−F^i,j+1/2 y−F^i,j−1/2 yΔy,F^i+1/2,j x=F(x)(qi,j,qi+1,j),F^i,j+1/2 y=F(y)(qi,j,qi,j+1).\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol q_{i,j}&=(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)^{\mathsf T}_{i,j},\\ \frac{d\boldsymbol q_{i,j}}{dt} &=-\frac{\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,x}_{i+1/2,j}-\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,x}_{i-1/2,j}}{\Delta x} -\frac{\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,y}_{i,j+1/2}-\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,y}_{i,j-1/2}}{\Delta y},\\ \widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,x}_{i+1/2,j} &=\boldsymbol F^{(x)}(\boldsymbol q_{i,j},\boldsymbol q_{i+1,j}),\\ \widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,y}_{i,j+1/2} &=\boldsymbol F^{(y)}(\boldsymbol q_{i,j},\boldsymbol q_{i,j+1}). \end{aligned}qi,j​dtdqi,j​​Fi+1/2,jx​Fi,j+1/2y​​=(ρ,ρu,ρv,E)i,jT​,=−ΔxFi+1/2,jx​−Fi−1/2,jx​​−ΔyFi,j+1/2y​−Fi,j−1/2y​​,=F(x)(qi,j​,qi+1,j​),=F(y)(qi,j​,qi,j+1​).​

Here F(x) and F(y) are the two-point flux above with normal velocity u and v, respectively. In the public numart/keep.py, rhs() forms the right flux with keep_flux(q, np.roll(q, -1, axis=axis), axis) and subtracts the left flux obtained with np.roll(flux, 1, axis=axis). The implementation uses a square periodic uniform grid with Δx = Δy = L/N. This is a nearest-neighbor, second-order central spatial operator; higher-order multi-point assembly and curvilinear forms are not implemented.

Both neighboring nodes share the same face flux. Its differences telescope when summed over the periodic grid, conserving total mass, momentum and total energy. RK4 retains these linear conservation laws, apart from roundoff. Kinetic-energy pressure-work balance and entropy involve nonlinear relations: this spatial formula alone does not guarantee their exact preservation after time discretization.

Tests verify equal-state consistency, left/right symmetry, periodic conservation of the primary variables, and the semidiscrete kinetic-energy pressure-work identity. RK4 adds temporal truncation error and does not inherit every semidiscrete identity exactly.

Appropriate first test: smooth isentropic vortex advection#

Use gamma = 1.4 on a periodic [−10,10]² square, background velocity (1,0.5), and vortex strength beta = 3. Relative to its translating center, write X, Y and r² = X²+Y². The reference is

u=1−βY2πe(1−r2)/2,v=0.5+βX2πe(1−r2)/2,T=1−(γ−1)β28γπ2e1−r2,ρ=T1/(γ−1),p=ργ. \begin{aligned} u&=1-\frac{\beta Y}{2\pi}e^{(1-r^2)/2},& v&=0.5+\frac{\beta X}{2\pi}e^{(1-r^2)/2},\\ T&=1-\frac{(\gamma-1)\beta^2}{8\gamma\pi^2}e^{1-r^2},& \rho&=T^{1/(\gamma-1)},\qquad p=\rho^\gamma. \end{aligned} uT​=1−2πβY​e(1−r2)/2,=1−8γπ2(γ−1)β2​e1−r2,​vρ​=0.5+2πβX​e(1−r2)/2,=T1/(γ−1),p=ργ.​

The whole-plane vortex translates analytically. Minimum-image coordinates in a box of length 20 leave its boundary tails below floating-point significance for these parameters, avoiding an appreciable periodic boundary jump. Positivity of the analytic temperature is checked explicitly.

The static study runs 48², 96², and 192² cells to t = 1 at CFL 0.25. It reports pointwise density L1 error, primary-conservation drift, and the change in S_* per mass. A separate 96² run halves the CFL to assess the time-step contribution. The measurement manifest states the actual errors; the expected smooth spatial order is two. The movie instead runs 96² to t = 4 and is a qualitative time-history exhibit, not a substitute for refinement.

Appropriate next tests#

A compressible inviscid 3D Taylor–Green vortex is a primary KEEP benchmark. Fix the periodic box [0,2 pi]³, reference density, gamma, initial Mach number, velocity amplitude, initial pressure perturbation, and temporal scheme. Track total energy, kinetic energy, pressure dilatation, entropy, density/temperature RMS fluctuations, and resolved spectra under grid and time refinement. Pressure-work exchange must be separated from numerical dissipation. The original paper also examines inviscid isotropic turbulence. These 3D computations are not implemented in this edition; a 2D slice is not a replacement for the three-dimensional vortex-stretching dynamics.

A smooth constant-pressure density wave tests preservation of pressure/velocity equilibrium. It is a useful additional diagnostic, but that property depends on the flux variant. Our original arithmetic-mean flux produces a nonzero semidiscrete pressure residual for this data; a test records that limitation instead of claiming it passes. Shima et al. (2021) study pressure-equilibrium-preserving split forms.

Caveats: entropy, shocks, and the scheme version#

  1. Preserving is not a roundoff-exact entropy guarantee. The authors explicitly distinguish improved entropy behavior from exact conservation in their 2022 explanation. Tamaki, Kuya, and Kawai (2022) analyze remaining entropy errors and redefine KEEP variants with tunable approximation order. Our code does not implement that redefinition or an exact logarithmic-mean entropy-conservative flux.
  2. A shock requires entropy production. A bare non-dissipative central flux supplies no general mechanism to select a non-oscillatory admissible shock solution. Sod, Shu–Osher, and Double Mach Reflection are not acceptance tests for the unstabilized baseline demonstrated here. A shock-tube run can still be a deliberate limitation/oscillation diagnostic; it should not be advertised as a validated shock-capturing result. It is inaccurate to turn this into a universal statement that every KEEP-based method is unable to solve any shock tube.
  3. Shock stabilization changes the method. A sensor, artificial viscosity, upwind blending, filtering, or another entropy-stable construction needs its own definition and tests. Record shock/contact thickness, oscillations, positivity, and added dissipation; keep separate results for the smooth base scheme and stabilized extension. No such extension is implemented here.
  4. No positivity theorem is supplied. Positive density and pressure in the displayed smooth runs do not establish robustness near vacuum or under arbitrary under-resolution. The code stops on nonphysical states rather than silently clipping them.
  5. Time integration and boundaries matter. Asada, Maruyama, and Kawai (2024) address temporal preservation. Our RK4, periodic uniform-grid experiment does not implement their method, high-order/curvilinear KEEP, hanging-node treatments, wall models, or a production LES solver.
