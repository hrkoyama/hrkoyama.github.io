KEEP schemes: preserve the smooth-flow balance, test the limits

KEEP stands for kinetic-energy and entropy preserving. The work of Yuichi Kuya, Kosuke Totani, and Soshi Kawai at Tohoku University seeks a low-dissipation balance between the conservative equations of compressible flow. The original 2018 paper is the starting point. The authors’ 2022 conference explanation, especially §2.2 and Eq. (9), states the Cartesian flux implemented here.

Which quantities should be preserved?#

In a smooth periodic inviscid flow, mass, momentum, and total energy are conservative. The total kinetic energy alone is generally not constant in compressible flow: pressure work exchanges kinetic and internal energy. With K = integral rho |u|²/2,

dKdt=∫p ∇⋅u dV. \frac{dK}{dt}=\int p\,\nabla\cdot u\,dV. dtdK​=∫p∇⋅udV.

A kinetic-energy-consistent convective discretization should not create a spurious kinetic-energy source through an inconsistent mass/momentum product rule. This is different from imposing dK/dt = 0 on a compressible vortex.

We measure the dimensionless entropy proxy

S∗=∫ρlog⁡(pργ)dV. S_* = \int\rho\log\left(\frac{p}{\rho^\gamma}\right)dV. S∗​=∫ρlog(ργp​)dV.

For a fixed ideal-gas composition this differs from physical entropy only by fixed factors and an additive mass term. Its change per initial mass is useful when initial isentropic data give S_* approximately zero; dividing by initial S_* would be ill-conditioned.

For the grid update formula, see the semidiscrete update and two-point flux.

The semidiscrete update and two-point flux#

Let an overbar denote the arithmetic mean of left and right values, u_n the velocity normal to a Cartesian face, and e = p/[rho(gamma−1)]. The baseline flux is

C=ρ‾ u‾n,Fρui=C u‾i+p‾ δin,FE=C[12uL⋅uR+e‾]+12(un,LpR+un,RpL). \begin{aligned} C&=\overline\rho\,\overline u_n,\\ F_{\rho u_i}&=C\,\overline u_i+\overline p\,\delta_{in},\\ F_E&=C\left[\frac12\boldsymbol u_L\cdot\boldsymbol u_R+\overline e\right] +\frac12(u_{n,L}p_R+u_{n,R}p_L). \end{aligned} CFρui​​FE​​=ρ​un​,=Cui​+p​δin​,=C[21​uL​⋅uR​+e]+21​(un,L​pR​+un,R​pL​).​

The mass flux is C. The cross-velocity kinetic term and cross-pressure work are deliberate; replacing them by a convenient average of the full physical flux changes the discrete energy exchange. The implementation is a central two-point flux-difference method of second spatial order. It has no Riemann-solver dissipation.

1. Distinguish the two discrete operators#

Work first in one space dimension on a periodic uniform grid of spacing h, with positive density. The left and right states at a face are the two neighboring nodal values. A bar is their arithmetic mean and a jump is right minus left. D differences a face flux; D₀ is the centered derivative of a nodal field.

aˉi+1/2=ai+ai+12,[a]i+1/2=ai+1−ai,(DF)i=Fi+1/2−Fi−1/2h,(D0a)i=ai+1−ai−12h.\begin{aligned}\bar a_{i+1/2}&=\frac{a_i+a_{i+1}}2,\qquad [a]_{i+1/2}=a_{i+1}-a_i,\\(D\mathcal F)_i&=\frac{\mathcal F_{i+1/2}-\mathcal F_{i-1/2}}h,\\(D_0a)_i&=\frac{a_{i+1}-a_{i-1}}{2h}.\end{aligned}aˉi+1/2​(DF)i​(D0​a)i​​=2ai​+ai+1​​,[a]i+1/2​=ai+1​−ai​,=hFi+1/2​−Fi−1/2​​,=2hai+1​−ai−1​​.​

Two exact algebraic identities explain why the particular averages matter:

[ab]=aˉ[b]+bˉ[a],ab‾=aˉ bˉ+14[a][b].[ab]=\bar a[b]+\bar b[a],\qquad \overline{ab}=\bar a\,\bar b+\frac14[a][b].[ab]=aˉ[b]+bˉ[a],ab=aˉbˉ+41​[a][b].

For example, averaging ρu is different from multiplying the separate means of ρ and u. They agree for equal states and to first order about a constant state, but their nonlinear jump terms differ. KEEP chooses the products of means to make the equations exchange energy consistently. Conservation of the summed total energy alone would not determine these products.

2. Derive kinetic energy from mass and momentum#

Let m denote momentum per unit volume. Use the mass flux C and momentum flux M below; each face has one shared flux. The full two-dimensional flux was given above. Here the velocity is a scalar to expose the cancellation.

mi=ρiui,ρ˙i=−(DC)i,m˙i=−(DM)i,C=ρˉ uˉ,M=Cuˉ+pˉ.\begin{aligned}m_i&=\rho_i u_i,\qquad\dot\rho_i=-(DC)_i,\qquad\dot m_i=-(DM)_i,\\C&=\bar\rho\,\bar u,\qquad M=C\bar u+\bar p.\end{aligned}mi​C​=ρi​ui​,ρ˙​i​=−(DC)i​,m˙i​=−(DM)i​,=ρˉ​uˉ,M=Cuˉ+pˉ​.​

Time is still continuous, so the ordinary chain rule applies to K=m²/(2ρ). Substituting the two conservative equations gives a kinetic-energy flux 𝒦 and a pressure-gradient term:

Ki=mi22ρi=12ρiui2,K˙i=uim˙i−12ui2ρ˙i=−(DK)i−ui(D0p)i,Ki+1/2=12Ci+1/2uiui+1.\begin{aligned} K_i&=\frac{m_i^2}{2\rho_i}=\frac12\rho_i u_i^2,\\ \dot K_i&=u_i\dot m_i-\frac12u_i^2\dot\rho_i =-(D\mathcal K)_i-u_i(D_0p)_i,\\ \mathcal K_{i+1/2}&=\frac12 C_{i+1/2}u_i u_{i+1}. \end{aligned}Ki​K˙i​Ki+1/2​​=2ρi​mi2​​=21​ρi​ui2​,=ui​m˙i​−21​ui2​ρ˙​i​=−(DK)i​−ui​(D0​p)i​,=21​Ci+1/2​ui​ui+1​.​

The advective cancellation at the right face is explicit:

ui(Ci+1/2ui+ui+12)−12ui2Ci+1/2=12uiui+1Ci+1/2.u_i\left(C_{i+1/2}\frac{u_i+u_{i+1}}2\right) -\frac12u_i^2 C_{i+1/2} =\frac12u_i u_{i+1}C_{i+1/2}.ui​(Ci+1/2​2ui​+ui+1​​)−21​ui2​Ci+1/2​=21​ui​ui+1​Ci+1/2​.

The left face cancels in the same way. Also D applied to the face mean of p is D₀p. Summing over the periodic grid cancels all shared kinetic-energy fluxes; discrete summation by parts then gives

ddt(h∑iKi)=−h∑iui(D0p)i=h∑ipi(D0u)i.\frac{d}{dt}\left(h\sum_i K_i\right) =-h\sum_i u_i(D_0p)_i =h\sum_i p_i(D_0u)_i.dtd​(hi∑​Ki​)=−hi∑​ui​(D0​p)i​=hi∑​pi​(D0​u)i​.

The sign is positive in the last expression: pressure dilatation transfers energy between kinetic and internal energy. Thus compressible-flow kinetic energy is generally not constant. The identity excludes a spurious advective source while retaining that physical exchange. In multiple dimensions, replace uLuR by the velocity dot product and sum the directional pressure work.

3. Recover the internal-energy pressure work#

Choose the pressure contribution P to the total-energy flux as a cross product of left and right values. Expanding its face difference gives an exact discrete product rule:

Pi+1/2=12(uipi+1+ui+1pi),(DP)i=ui(pi+1−pi−1)+pi(ui+1−ui−1)2h=ui(D0p)i+pi(D0u)i.\begin{aligned} P_{i+1/2}&=\frac12(u_i p_{i+1}+u_{i+1}p_i),\\ (DP)_i&=\frac{u_i(p_{i+1}-p_{i-1})+p_i(u_{i+1}-u_{i-1})}{2h}\\ &=u_i(D_0p)_i+p_i(D_0u)_i. \end{aligned}Pi+1/2​(DP)i​​=21​(ui​pi+1​+ui+1​pi​),=2hui​(pi+1​−pi−1​)+pi​(ui+1​−ui−1​)​=ui​(D0​p)i​+pi​(D0​u)i​.​

Use the same mass flux to transport the arithmetic mean of the specific internal energy. Subtracting the derived kinetic-energy equation from the total-energy equation then yields

Ii=ρiei=Ei−Ki,ei=piρi(γ−1),Ii+1/2=Ci+1/2eˉi+1/2,FE=K+I+P,E˙i+(DFE)i=0⟹I˙i+(DI)i+pi(D0u)i=0.\begin{aligned} I_i&=\rho_i e_i=E_i-K_i,\qquad e_i=\frac{p_i}{\rho_i(\gamma-1)},\\ \mathcal I_{i+1/2}&=C_{i+1/2}\bar e_{i+1/2},\qquad F^E=\mathcal K+\mathcal I+P,\\ \dot E_i+(DF^E)_i&=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dot I_i+(D\mathcal I)_i+p_i(D_0u)_i=0. \end{aligned}Ii​Ii+1/2​E˙i​+(DFE)i​​=ρi​ei​=Ei​−Ki​,ei​=ρi​(γ−1)pi​​,=Ci+1/2​eˉi+1/2​,FE=K+I+P,=0⟹I˙i​+(DI)i​+pi​(D0​u)i​=0.​

The unwanted uD₀p terms cancel, leaving the internal-energy pressure work pD₀u with the required sign. This explains the cross-pressure term and the cross-velocity kinetic term in the implemented flux. They are not an arbitrary average of the physical energy flux. These are independent algebraic steps for the published implementation, corresponding to the second-order formulation in §2.2, Eqs. (8)–(12).

This derivation does not prove roundoff-exact entropy conservation. Entropy also involves nonlinear thermodynamic chain rules. The arithmetic-mean 2018 baseline preserves the balances above but retains entropy-conservation error; later KEEP variants and logarithmic-mean entropy-conservative fluxes have different definitions. The next formula returns to the actual two-dimensional update used by the code.

4. The implemented two-dimensional update

Evaluate this two-point flux between neighboring grid nodes, then take the right-minus-left flux difference to update the conserved state. Written in the two-dimensional notation of this implementation, Kuya and Kawai (2022), §2.2, Eqs. (1), (8) and (9) give the following semidiscrete system: space is discrete while time remains continuous. E is total energy per unit volume.

qi,j=(ρ,ρu,ρv,E)i,jT,dqi,jdt=−F^i+1/2,j x−F^i−1/2,j xΔx−F^i,j+1/2 y−F^i,j−1/2 yΔy,F^i+1/2,j x=F(x)(qi,j,qi+1,j),F^i,j+1/2 y=F(y)(qi,j,qi,j+1).\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol q_{i,j}&=(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)^{\mathsf T}_{i,j},\\ \frac{d\boldsymbol q_{i,j}}{dt} &=-\frac{\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,x}_{i+1/2,j}-\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,x}_{i-1/2,j}}{\Delta x} -\frac{\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,y}_{i,j+1/2}-\widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,y}_{i,j-1/2}}{\Delta y},\\ \widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,x}_{i+1/2,j} &=\boldsymbol F^{(x)}(\boldsymbol q_{i,j},\boldsymbol q_{i+1,j}),\\ \widehat{\boldsymbol F}^{\,y}_{i,j+1/2} &=\boldsymbol F^{(y)}(\boldsymbol q_{i,j},\boldsymbol q_{i,j+1}). \end{aligned}qi,j​dtdqi,j​​Fi+1/2,jx​Fi,j+1/2y​​=(ρ,ρu,ρv,E)i,jT​,=−ΔxFi+1/2,jx​−Fi−1/2,jx​​−ΔyFi,j+1/2y​−Fi,j−1/2y​​,=F(x)(qi,j​,qi+1,j​),=F(y)(qi,j​,qi,j+1​).​

Here F(x) and F(y) are the two-point flux above with normal velocity u and v, respectively. In the public numart/keep.py, rhs() forms the right flux with keep_flux(q, np.roll(q, -1, axis=axis), axis) and subtracts the left flux obtained with np.roll(flux, 1, axis=axis). The implementation uses a square periodic uniform grid with Δx = Δy = L/N. This is a nearest-neighbor, second-order central spatial operator; higher-order multi-point assembly and curvilinear forms are not implemented.

Both neighboring nodes share the same face flux. Its differences telescope when summed over the periodic grid, conserving total mass, momentum and total energy. RK4 retains these linear conservation laws, apart from roundoff. Kinetic-energy pressure-work balance and entropy involve nonlinear relations: this spatial formula alone does not guarantee their exact preservation after time discretization.

Tests verify equal-state consistency, left/right symmetry, periodic conservation of the primary variables, and the semidiscrete kinetic-energy pressure-work identity. RK4 adds temporal truncation error and does not inherit every semidiscrete identity exactly.

Non-dissipative does not mean dispersion-free#

Dissipation changes amplitude; dispersion changes phase speed. A spatial operator can avoid artificial damping and still move different wavelengths at incorrect speeds. To isolate that distinction, linearize the symmetric, consistent two-point flux about one uniform state q₀. Symmetry and F(q,q)=f(q) give equal left and right Jacobians. For a scalar characteristic wave with speed a, the result is the centered advection equation:

The von Neumann foundations explain the general procedure for reading dissipation and dispersion from an amplification factor’s magnitude and argument. Here that procedure is applied to KEEP linearized about a uniform state.

∂1F(q0,q0)=∂2F(q0,q0)=12A,A=f′(q0),v˙i=−avi+1−vi−12h.\begin{aligned}\partial_1 F(q_0,q_0)&=\partial_2 F(q_0,q_0)=\tfrac12 A,\qquad A=f'(q_0),\\\dot v_i&=-a\frac{v_{i+1}-v_{i-1}}{2h}.\end{aligned}∂1​F(q0​,q0​)v˙i​​=∂2​F(q0​,q0​)=21​A,A=f′(q0​),=−a2hvi+1​−vi−1​​.​

For an individual Fourier mode, with θ=kh, substitution gives

vi(t)=v^ ei(kih−ωht),θ=kh,λh=−i asin⁡θh,ωh=asin⁡θh.\begin{aligned}v_i(t)&=\widehat v\,e^{\mathrm i(ki h-\omega_h t)},\qquad\theta=kh,\\\lambda_h&=-\mathrm i\,a\frac{\sin\theta}{h},\qquad\omega_h=a\frac{\sin\theta}{h}.\end{aligned}vi​(t)λh​​=vei(kih−ωh​t),θ=kh,=−iahsinθ​,ωh​=ahsinθ​.​

The eigenvalue is purely imaginary, so this semidiscrete linear mode has no amplitude decay. Its phase and group velocities are nevertheless

cpha=ωhak=sin⁡θθ,cga=1adωhdk=cos⁡θ,sin⁡θθ=1−θ26+O(θ4).\begin{aligned}\frac{c_{\rm ph}}a&=\frac{\omega_h}{ak}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta},\\\frac{c_{\rm g}}a&=\frac1a\frac{d\omega_h}{dk}=\cos\theta,\\\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}&=1-\frac{\theta^2}{6}+O(\theta^4).\end{aligned}acph​​acg​​θsinθ​​=akωh​​=θsinθ​,=a1​dkdωh​​=cosθ,=1−6θ2​+O(θ4).​

The ratio at θ=0 is understood by continuity. At four grid points per wavelength, θ=π/2, the second-order phase speed is only 2/π≈0.637 times a. At θ=π the centered derivative vanishes on the alternating grid mode; that is a resolution failure, not accurate transport. Underresolved wave packets can also have an incorrect group velocity.

The speed ratios assume a≠0. When a=0, this linearization has ωh=0 and the mode is stationary.

The sine relation is not a general nonlinear KEEP dispersion law. Finite-amplitude products couple modes and introduce aliasing questions. Kuya and Kawai’s 2022 split-form analysis treats that different problem: its nonlinear modified wavenumber cannot be replaced by this linearized example, and a finite-difference split form does not automatically remove aliasing. The authors also identify dispersion-error reduction as a reason for higher-order KEEP formulations (§2.4).

What changes when RK4 advances time?#

The displayed solver uses classical four-stage RK4. For a frozen linear mode with eigenvalue −iωh, one step multiplies the amplitude by G. This follows directly from the RK4 stages; it is an analysis of the integrator used here, not of a later fully conservative temporal KEEP scheme.

vn+1=Gvn,G=R(−iξ),ξ=ωhΔt,R(z)=1+z+z22+z36+z424.\begin{aligned}v^{n+1}&=Gv^n,\qquad G=R(-\mathrm i\xi),\qquad\xi=\omega_h\Delta t,\\R(z)&=1+z+\frac{z^2}{2}+\frac{z^3}{6}+\frac{z^4}{24}.\end{aligned}vn+1R(z)​=Gvn,G=R(−iξ),ξ=ωh​Δt,=1+z+2z2​+6z3​+24z4​.​

Its squared amplitude gain is exactly

∣G∣2=1−ξ672+ξ8576,∣G∣≤1 ⟺ ∣ξ∣≤22(ξ∈R).\begin{gathered}|G|^2=1-\frac{\xi^6}{72}+\frac{\xi^8}{576},\\|G|\le1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ |\xi|\le2\sqrt2\quad(\xi\in\mathbb R).\end{gathered}∣G∣2=1−72ξ6​+576ξ8​,∣G∣≤1 ⟺ ∣ξ∣≤22​(ξ∈R).​

Strictly inside this interval, a nonzero ξ gives |G| smaller than one. Thus RK4 damps this otherwise undamped spatial mode. Gain is one at ξ=0 and at the two endpoints; beyond them it exceeds one. This pure-imaginary linear-mode stability limit is not a global CFL or positivity guarantee for the nonlinear, variable-state, two-dimensional solver.

Phase must also be checked. Define arg₀ by continuous continuation from ξ=0 rather than by a wrapped principal angle. The fully discrete frequency Ω and the amplitude after n equal steps satisfy

ΩΔt=−arg⁡0R(−iξ),∣vn∣=∣G∣n∣v0∣,ΩΔt=ξ−ξ5120+O(ξ7).\begin{aligned}\Omega\Delta t&=-\arg_0R(-\mathrm i\xi),\qquad |v^n|=|G|^n|v^0|,\\\Omega\Delta t&=\xi-\frac{\xi^5}{120}+O(\xi^7).\end{aligned}ΩΔtΩΔt​=−arg0​R(−iξ),∣vn∣=∣G∣n∣v0∣,=ξ−120ξ5​+O(ξ7).​

For small ξ the leading amplitude defect and the combined spatial/temporal phase-speed ratio are

log⁡∣G∣=−ξ6144+O(ξ8),ΩΔtakΔt=sin⁡θθ −arg⁡0R(−iξ)ξ.\begin{aligned}\log|G|&=-\frac{\xi^6}{144}+O(\xi^8),\\\frac{\Omega\Delta t}{a k\Delta t}&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}\,\frac{-\arg_0R(-\mathrm i\xi)}{\xi}.\end{aligned}log∣G∣akΔtΩΔt​​=−144ξ6​+O(ξ8),=θsinθ​ξ−arg0​R(−iξ)​.​

For θ=π/2 and aΔt/h=0.5, the spatial phase-speed ratio is about 0.636620; including RK4 gives about 0.636317. The RK4 gain is about 0.999894878 per step and 0.989542 after 100 steps. These are calculations for one linear mode.

The small-ξ expansion shows phase lag near zero, but RK4 does not lag throughout its stable interval. At ξ=2, for example, its continuous phase is about 2.0344, ahead of the exact value 2.

The first factor in the last expression is spatial dispersion. The second is the additional RK4 phase error, with its limit taken at ξ=0. The accompanying response plot is for the scalar linear test equation; its amplitude is not a measured entropy error of the vortex.

There is no contradiction with conservation of the summed primary variables. If the discrete right-hand side has zero summed mass, momentum and total-energy residual at every stage, RK4 preserves those linear sums, apart from roundoff. K=m²/(2ρ), entropy, wave amplitudes and phases are nonlinear or more detailed properties; preserving the sums does not fix them. See the Runge–Kutta chapter for the stages and stability-function context.

Read the diagnostics separately#

  1. At fixed grid spacing, reduce Δt. Changes in amplitude and phase expose temporal error; the spatial dispersion remains.
  2. Refine the grid as well, with sufficiently small temporal error. Refinement at fixed CFL changes both h and Δt, so it alone does not separate their contributions.
  3. For small perturbations of a uniform state, compare one mode with its exact amplitude and phase. For a nonlinear vortex, compare fields and pressure-work/entropy diagnostics instead of applying the scalar sine relation to the whole flow.
  4. Track primary conservation, kinetic-energy pressure-work balance, entropy-proxy change, density/pressure positivity, and field error separately. A dispersive oscillation is not automatically growing instability; conversely, actual growth cannot be dismissed as harmless dispersion.

The two response figures are analytical diagnostics, not additional atlas test problems. They do not change the published solver, fixed inputs, saved vortex results or code-edition ZIP. The existing vortex study below supplies the separate finite-grid evidence.

Appropriate first test: smooth isentropic vortex advection#

Use gamma = 1.4 on a periodic [−10,10]² square, background velocity (1,0.5), and vortex strength beta = 3. Relative to its translating center, write X, Y and r² = X²+Y². The reference is

u=1−βY2πe(1−r2)/2,v=0.5+βX2πe(1−r2)/2,T=1−(γ−1)β28γπ2e1−r2,ρ=T1/(γ−1),p=ργ. \begin{aligned} u&=1-\frac{\beta Y}{2\pi}e^{(1-r^2)/2},& v&=0.5+\frac{\beta X}{2\pi}e^{(1-r^2)/2},\\ T&=1-\frac{(\gamma-1)\beta^2}{8\gamma\pi^2}e^{1-r^2},& \rho&=T^{1/(\gamma-1)},\qquad p=\rho^\gamma. \end{aligned} uT​=1−2πβY​e(1−r2)/2,=1−8γπ2(γ−1)β2​e1−r2,​vρ​=0.5+2πβX​e(1−r2)/2,=T1/(γ−1),p=ργ.​

The whole-plane vortex translates analytically. Minimum-image coordinates in a box of length 20 leave its boundary tails below floating-point significance for these parameters, avoiding an appreciable periodic boundary jump. Positivity of the analytic temperature is checked explicitly.

The static study runs 48², 96², and 192² cells to t = 1 at CFL 0.25. It reports pointwise density L1 error, primary-conservation drift, and the change in S_* per mass. A separate 96² run halves the CFL to assess the time-step contribution. The measurement manifest states the actual errors; the expected smooth spatial order is two. The movie instead runs 96² to t = 4 and is a qualitative time-history exhibit, not a substitute for refinement.

Appropriate next tests#

A compressible inviscid 3D Taylor–Green vortex is a primary KEEP benchmark. Fix the periodic box [0,2 pi]³, reference density, gamma, initial Mach number, velocity amplitude, initial pressure perturbation, and temporal scheme. Track total energy, kinetic energy, pressure dilatation, entropy, density/temperature RMS fluctuations, and resolved spectra under grid and time refinement. Pressure-work exchange must be separated from numerical dissipation. The original paper also examines inviscid isotropic turbulence. These 3D computations are not implemented in this edition; a 2D slice is not a replacement for the three-dimensional vortex-stretching dynamics.

A smooth constant-pressure density wave tests preservation of pressure/velocity equilibrium. It is a useful additional diagnostic, but that property depends on the flux variant. Our original arithmetic-mean flux produces a nonzero semidiscrete pressure residual for this data; a test records that limitation instead of claiming it passes. Shima et al. (2021) study pressure-equilibrium-preserving split forms.

Caveats: entropy, shocks, and the scheme version#

  1. Preserving is not a roundoff-exact entropy guarantee. The authors explicitly distinguish improved entropy behavior from exact conservation in their 2022 explanation. Tamaki, Kuya, and Kawai (2022) analyze remaining entropy errors and redefine KEEP variants with tunable approximation order. Our code does not implement that redefinition or an exact logarithmic-mean entropy-conservative flux.
  2. A shock requires entropy production. A bare non-dissipative central flux supplies no general mechanism to select a non-oscillatory admissible shock solution. Sod, Shu–Osher, and Double Mach Reflection are not acceptance tests for the unstabilized baseline demonstrated here. A shock-tube run can still be a deliberate limitation/oscillation diagnostic; it should not be advertised as a validated shock-capturing result. It is inaccurate to turn this into a universal statement that every KEEP-based method is unable to solve any shock tube.
  3. Shock stabilization changes the method. A sensor, artificial viscosity, upwind blending, filtering, or another entropy-stable construction needs its own definition and tests. Record shock/contact thickness, oscillations, positivity, and added dissipation; keep separate results for the smooth base scheme and stabilized extension. No such extension is implemented here.
  4. No positivity theorem is supplied. Positive density and pressure in the displayed smooth runs do not establish robustness near vacuum or under arbitrary under-resolution. The code stops on nonphysical states rather than silently clipping them.
  5. Time integration and boundaries matter. Asada, Maruyama, and Kawai (2024) address temporal preservation. Our RK4, periodic uniform-grid experiment does not implement their method, high-order/curvilinear KEEP, hanging-node treatments, wall models, or a production LES solver.

python scripts/build_keep.py
python -m pip install '.[movies]'
python scripts/build_movies.py

The first command creates the smooth-error figure and its source manifest. The second build adds the KEEP vortex to the motion gallery.
