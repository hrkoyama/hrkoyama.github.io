Preconditioning, dual time stepping, and residual distribution

Condition numbers and PDE preconditioning#

For Ax=b, the 2-norm condition number is κ₂(A)=σmax/σmin. It measures sensitivity to perturbations rather than the convergence rate of a particular iteration. Left preconditioning gives P^{-1}Ax=P^{-1}b; right preconditioning gives AP^{-1}y=b, x=P^{-1}y. For symmetric positive-definite systems, P^{-1/2}AP^{-1/2} supports symmetry-preserving comparisons.

conditioning_example constructs A=S B S, where B is a 1D Poisson matrix and S is diagonal scaling. It demonstrates the change in condition number under symmetric Jacobi scaling. It does not implement Turkel's low-Mach flow preconditioning matrix.

Compressible PDEs pose a separate preconditioning problem when low-Mach convective and acoustic characteristic speeds differ greatly. A pseudo-time system such as Γ(U) ∂U/∂τ + R(U)=0 modifies characteristic speeds, convergence, and numerical dissipation. Deriving Γ, transforming variables, choosing Mach-dependent coefficients, and maintaining boundary consistency require additional verification. Replacing the physical time derivative indiscriminately changes the time-dependent solution. Turkel (1987).

Dual time stepping#

Applying BDF2 in physical time to dU/dt+R(U)=0 gives

3Un+1−4Un+Un−12Δt+R(Un+1)=0. \frac{3U^{n+1}-4U^n+U^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+R(U^{n+1})=0. 2Δt3Un+1−4Un+Un−1​+R(Un+1)=0.

At each physical step, evolve the pseudo-time equation for Q,

dQdτ+3Q−4Un+Un−12Δt+R(Q)=0, \frac{dQ}{d\tau}+\frac{3Q-4U^n+U^{n-1}}{2\Delta t}+R(Q)=0, dτdQ​+2Δt3Q−4Un+Un−1​+R(Q)=0,

to a steady state. Backward Euler starts the first step. Physical-time accuracy depends on Δt, spatial error, and incomplete inner convergence. A large physical CFL alone does not establish accurate time evolution. Jameson (1991).

dual_time_advection is a minimal periodic 1D example: implicit upwind advection with BDF1/BDF2, solved by a Jacobi pseudo-time iteration. The inner residual threshold is tol*max(1,||b||∞), with default tol=1e-10. Failure to converge within the iteration limit raises an error. Tests compare against an independent dense solve of the BDF2 discrete equations. This is a reduced model rather than Jameson's complete compressible-flow multigrid algorithm.

Residual distribution schemes#

Distribute an element residual Φ_K=∫K ∇·F(U) dV to its nodes while preserving Σ_{i∈K} Φ_i^K=Φ_K. This conservation condition connects continuous finite elements with upwind ideas. Abgrall (2006), author-hosted review.

rd_residual is a minimal 1D P1 scalar-advection example. It assigns all of Φ_K=a(u_{i+1}-u_i) to the downstream node and divides by lumped mass h. Tests establish equivalence to first-order upwind differences in this case. 2D triangular N/LDA schemes, nonlinear blending, time-consistent mass matrices, and Euler systems remain unimplemented.

Roe, entropy fixes, Einfeldt, and rotated solvers#

Roe uses a linearization satisfying F_R-F_L=Ã(U_R-U_L) and its eigensystem. This edition forms sqrt(ρ)-weighted u and H, with ã²=(γ-1)(H̃-ũ²/2), and adds dissipation through wave strengths and eigenvectors. Roe (1981).

The entropy fix replaces the absolute acoustic eigenvalue below δ with (λ²+δ²)/(2δ). HLLE is a separate flux using Einfeldt-type left/right wave speeds for strong low-density expansions. Roe entropy correction, HLLE positivity properties, and carbuncle treatments address different problems. Einfeldt (1988), Einfeldt et al. (1991).

Rotated-hybrid Riemann solvers combine fluxes using directions beyond the face normal to balance shock robustness and shear-layer resolution. A coordinate rotation alone does not reproduce this construction. This edition adds a specified directional HLLE/Roe composition and cylinder-forebody tests. This explicit two-flux experiment is distinct from an optimized reproduction of the original Rotated-RHLL dissipation formula. Nishikawa–Kitamura (2008).

Roe's Property U#

Roe's 1981 paper, author-uploaded full text, pp. 359–360 uses four conditions: (i) a linear mapping from conserved-state space to flux space for each fixed state pair; (ii) the coincident-state limit equals the physical Jacobian; (iii) the matrix maps the state jump exactly onto the flux jump; (iv) its eigenvectors are linearly independent. The two-state matrix need not depend linearly on its arguments. For physical ideal-gas Euler states the construction below also has real acoustic/contact speeds.

Here is the algebra implemented and independently checked in this atlas. For U=(ρ,ρu,E), H=(E+p)/ρ, set z=√ρ(1,u,H). Then

U(z)=(z12z1z2(z1z3+(γ−1)z22/2)/γ),F(z)=(z1z2((γ+1)z22/2+(γ−1)z1z3)/γz2z3). U(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z_1^2\\z_1z_2\\ (z_1z_3+(\gamma-1)z_2^2/2)/\gamma\end{pmatrix},\qquad F(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z_1z_2\\ ((\gamma+1)z_2^2/2+(\gamma-1)z_1z_3)/\gamma\\z_2z_3\end{pmatrix}. U(z)=​z12​z1​z2​(z1​z3​+(γ−1)z22​/2)/γ​​,F(z)=​z1​z2​((γ+1)z22​/2+(γ−1)z1​z3​)/γz2​z3​​​.

Both maps are quadratic. Their midpoint Jacobians B=∂U/∂z and C=∂F/∂z therefore satisfy ΔU=B(z̄)Δz and ΔF=C(z̄)Δz exactly. Consequently Ã=C(z̄)B(z̄)⁻¹ has the jump property. Ratios of z̄ give

u~=ρLuL+ρRuRρL+ρR,H~=ρLHL+ρRHRρL+ρR,c~2=(γ−1)(H~−u~2/2). \widetilde u=\frac{\sqrt{\rho_L}u_L+\sqrt{\rho_R}u_R}{\sqrt{\rho_L}+\sqrt{\rho_R}},\quad \widetilde H=\frac{\sqrt{\rho_L}H_L+\sqrt{\rho_R}H_R}{\sqrt{\rho_L}+\sqrt{\rho_R}},\quad \widetilde c^2=(\gamma-1)(\widetilde H-\widetilde u^2/2). u=ρL​​+ρR​​ρL​​uL​+ρR​​uR​​,H=ρL​​+ρR​​ρL​​HL​+ρR​​HR​​,c2=(γ−1)(H−u2/2).

At equal states, the chain rule gives CB⁻¹=∂F/∂U. The independently constructed eigenbasis has speeds ũ−c̃,ũ,ũ+c̃. Since c̃>0, its three eigenvectors span the state space. We check the parameter-vector matrix against this eigenbasis, the analytic Jacobian, and 2000 deterministic physical state pairs.

If ΔF=sΔU for a single nonzero Rankine–Hugoniot jump, then ÃΔU=sΔU: the jump lies in the speed-s eigenspace. The unmodified Roe flux consequently recognizes isolated contact/shock jumps. This does not make every reconstructed, time-discretized shock calculation exact. Smoothing |λ| changes the flux without changing the matrix identity, and can smear a stationary shock.

An expansion discontinuity can also satisfy the jump relation. Our reversed stationary normal-shock pair passes Property U but the raw Roe flux differs from the entropy Riemann reference. This is a numerical counterexample to confusing the jump property with entropy selection. The entropy fix is an additional design choice; neither gives a multidimensional carbuncle guarantee.
