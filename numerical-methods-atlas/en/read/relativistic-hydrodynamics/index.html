Relativistic hydrodynamics: SRHD, GRHD, and GRMHD

Literature review, checked 6 November 2026. These methods are not implemented in the atlas's Newtonian Euler library. The development has several interacting branches: conservative shock capture, curved and evolving geometry, magnetic constraints, physical-state recovery, high-order methods, radiation, and portable parallel computation. Dates below distinguish publication from preprint and acceptance dates. This is a selected reading path, not an exhaustive review or a ranking of codes.

What changes from the existing Euler examples?#

SRHD evolves relativistic matter in flat spacetime. GRHD includes a curved metric, which may be prescribed or evolved with Einstein's equations. GRMHD also evolves the electromagnetic contribution. A Kerr-background accretion calculation and a binary-neutron-star calculation with dynamical spacetime therefore test different systems.

With c = G = 1 and metric signature (-,+,+,+), ideal-fluid equations are

∇μ(ρ0uμ)=0,∇μTμν=0,Tμν=ρ0huμuν+pgμν,h=1+ϵ+pρ0. \nabla_\mu(\rho_0 u^\mu)=0,\qquad \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad T^{\mu\nu}=\rho_0 h u^\mu u^\nu+p g^{\mu\nu},\qquad h=1+\epsilon+\frac{p}{\rho_0}. ∇μ​(ρ0​uμ)=0,∇μ​Tμν=0,Tμν=ρ0​huμuν+pgμν,h=1+ϵ+ρ0​p​.

Here rho0 is rest-mass density and epsilon is specific internal energy. In Cartesian SRHD, a common conservative state is

W=(1−v2)−1/2,D=ρ0W,Si=ρ0hW2vi,τ=ρ0hW2−p−D. W=(1-v^2)^{-1/2},\qquad D=\rho_0W,\quad S_i=\rho_0hW^2v_i,\quad \tau=\rho_0hW^2-p-D. W=(1−v2)−1/2,D=ρ0​W,Si​=ρ0​hW2vi​,τ=ρ0​hW2−p−D.

It differs from the Newtonian state in the existing benchmarks. Primitive recovery is nonlinear. One-dimensional acoustic speeds with zero transverse velocity are (v +/- cs)/(1 +/- v cs), not v +/- cs. In a 3+1 GR formulation, lapse, shift, spatial volume factors, and metric derivatives enter the fluxes and sources. These are structural changes, not a different value of gamma. Font (2008) is the formulation review used here; the original Valencia reference is below.

1972–1998: from curved-background flows to conservative HRSC#

Wilson (1972) is an early numerical study of gas falling into a Kerr black hole. It provides historical context for relativistic fluid computation, rather than a claim that fixed-background hydrodynamics solves the full evolving Einstein system.

The high-resolution shock-capturing (HRSC) branch brings the characteristic and conservative methods of classical CFD into relativity. Martí and Müller (1994) give an exact SR Riemann solution for flow normal to the initial discontinuity. That scope matters when selecting multidimensional tests with transverse velocities; the original normal-flow reference is not a general transverse Riemann solution.

Banyuls et al. (1997) present the 3+1 local characteristic formulation commonly called Valencia. Its conservative state, characteristic information, and geometric source terms make modern shock capture applicable in GR. It is a central reference, not a claim that all earlier relativistic methods were nonconservative. Pons et al. (1998) connect GRHD to SR Riemann solvers through a local coordinate transformation. Curvature and coordinate effects still have to be accounted for outside that local solve.

1999–2006: magnetic constraints and dynamical spacetime#

Komissarov (1999) develops a multidimensional Godunov-type relativistic MHD scheme with magnetic flux conservation. HARM, Gammie–McKinney–Tóth (2003) combines conservative GRMHD, HLL fluxes, and a Tóth-type CT construction, with black-hole torus calculations among its examples. Its original formulation must be distinguished from later dynamical-spacetime frameworks.

Duez et al. (2005) evolve the coupled Einstein–Maxwell–MHD system, using BSSN for spacetime and conservative shock capture for the matter. This is the additional step needed when matter and geometry affect one another dynamically.

Conservation variables do not remove the need to recover physical fluid states. Noble et al. (2006) compare nonlinear recovery algorithms, including reductions to one or two unknowns. Recovery failure, atmosphere floors, and repairs must be tracked separately from truncation error and conserved-variable residuals.

2011–2026: the particle branch from GSPH#

Inutsuka's original GSPH connects to Iwasaki–Inutsuka (2011), a Newtonian particle MHD method using Riemann problems and Alfvén characteristics. It is not itself relativistic.

Kitajima–Inutsuka–Seno (2026) then formulate special relativistic Godunov SPH (SRGSPH). The paper addresses convolution-based interactions, unequal baryon numbers, and variable smoothing lengths, with shock-tube and Kelvin–Helmholtz examples. It is a direct bridge between the requested particle and relativistic branches. The preprint is from October 2025; the journal issue is January 2026, despite the DOI's 2025 identifier. It should not be described as a GRHD implementation.

2013 onward: radiation and microphysics add stiff systems#

Sądowski et al. (2013) describe KORAL's covariant M1 radiation moments with semi-implicit interaction sources. This connects relativistic fluid methods to the atlas's planned implicit and IMEX chapters. M1 closes angular moments; it does not solve the full radiation distribution without approximation.

For neutron-star mergers, Foucart (2023) reviews leakage, moment, and Monte Carlo neutrino transport and the remaining interaction-modeling limitations. Tabulated finite-temperature equations of state, composition, transport, and their uncertainties are separate advances from the choice of a Riemann flux. Ideal relativistic hydrodynamics also does not automatically supply a causal relativistic viscous theory.

A parallel 1979–2019 branch: causal viscous fluid theory#

The Newtonian viscous stress cannot simply be inserted into relativistic Euler equations without examining causality and the initial-value problem. Israel–Stewart (1979) provides a central reference for transient dissipative theories with additional relaxation variables.

Bemfica–Disconzi–Noronha (2019) and Kovtun (2019) develop a different line: appropriate first-gradient constitutive choices and hydrodynamic frames can support causal or stable formulations under specified conditions. The former paper addresses nonlinear causality without extra extended variables; the latter examines stable first-order frames. Their results should not be merged into an unconditional claim for every equation of state or transport choice. Physical causality, PDE well-posedness, and stability of a discrete time step are separate checks. This branch is theory and reading context in the atlas, not an implemented viscous relativistic solver.

2022–2025: high order with shocks, surfaces, and physical constraints#

Deppe et al. (2022) develop a DG–finite difference hybrid: attempt a high-order DG update and recompute inadmissible regions with robust FD. The 2021 preprint and 2022 publication dates should remain distinct. Their binary-neutron-star study (2024 preprint) extends the hybrid approach to dynamical spacetime and merger calculations. The latter is cited here as a preprint; a separate journal record has not been verified in this review.

Cao–Peng–Wu (2025) study physical-constraint-preserving, oscillation-eliminating DG for GRHD, including positive thermodynamic states and subluminal velocity under their stated scheme assumptions. Their results are not a guarantee for arbitrary limiters, fluxes, EOS, or spacetime discretizations. A theorem for a selected GRHD formulation and an astrophysical merger-code demonstration are different types of evidence.

2025–2026: portable GPU computation of the coupled system#

AthenaK, Fields et al. (2025) demonstrates dynamical-spacetime GRMHD and binary-neutron-star mergers on GPUs, with CT and first-order flux correction among its numerical ingredients. Its preprint is from 2024; the journal reference is ApJS 276, 35 (2025). AsterX, Kalinani et al. (2025 issue) provides another open GPU-oriented GRMHD framework, based on Valencia and Z4c. Its online publication was December 2024. Refinement infrastructure is part of those source codes; implementing AMR in this atlas remains deferred.

GRACE, Musolino et al. was accepted by Physical Review D on 2 November 2026, as checked on 6 November. Its July 2026 preprint describes Kokkos-based portability, CT, Z4c coupling, and neutron-star merger examples. Acceptance is not a verified final volume/page citation. These papers establish examples of portable implementations; they do not establish a universal speed or accuracy ranking across hardware and physics.

What the atlas can reproduce in stages#

The following is an implementation proposal, not a result of this review:

  1. SRHD: primitive/conservative round trips, characteristic speeds, smooth waves, shock tubes, reflection, and transverse-velocity cases. Check EOS validity, rho and pressure positivity, and speeds below c.
  2. SRMHD: add relativistic Alfvén waves, magnetic shock tubes, and a rotor; specify the magnetic-divergence operator and every repair/floor.
  3. Fixed-metric GR: metric-aware flat-space limits, Michel/Bondi accretion, equilibrium stars, and torus configurations, with geometric source balance.
  4. Dynamical GR: couple a separately verified spacetime evolution; monitor Einstein constraints, baryon conservation, stellar oscillations, and waveform convergence before attempting mergers.
  5. Radiation or SRGSPH: keep each branch's state, boundary, conservation, transport closure, and reference tests explicit. Reuse the Newtonian examples as comparisons, not as evidence that a relativistic solver already exists.

The historical conclusion is that progress comes from the combination of these layers. A new high-order reconstruction or a larger GPU run alone does not resolve primitive recovery, surface/atmosphere handling, constraint errors, or microphysical uncertainty.

Equations

  • Special-relativistic hydrodynamics (SRHD)

    Ideal fluid in flat Cartesian spacetime,c=1. Add a mild fixed symmetric two-shock example with gamma-law EOS and primitive recovery; no general three-wave Riemann,extreme-Lorentz-factor or GR claim.

  • General-relativistic hydrodynamics (GRHD)

    Ideal fluid with c=G=1 and signature (−,+,+,+). Distinguish prescribed geometry from an evolved Einstein system. Literature-only.

  • General-relativistic MHD (GRMHD)

    Representative ideal-conductivity form combining matter/EM stress-energy, magnetic constraints and geometry. Consult the chapter for closure and spacetime choices. Literature-only.
