Runge–Kutta families: stages, order, stability, and SSP

RK2 and RK4 denote orders, while SSP describes a stability-inheritance property. These labels answer different questions. The Runge–Kutta family notebook compares nine explicit methods, their full stage coefficients, nonlinear predictors, linear stability contours, and measured refinement orders.

One step, several right-hand-side evaluations#

An explicit s-stage Runge–Kutta method for y′=f(t,y) is

Yi=yn+h∑j<iaijkj,ki=f(tn+cih,Yi),yn+1=yn+h∑i=1sbiki,ci=∑j<iaij. Y_i=y_n+h\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}k_j,\qquad k_i=f(t_n+c_i h,Y_i),\qquad y_{n+1}=y_n+h\sum_{i=1}^s b_i k_i, \qquad c_i=\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}. Yi​=yn​+hj<i∑​aij​kj​,ki​=f(tn​+ci​h,Yi​),yn+1​=yn​+hi=1∑s​bi​ki​,ci​=j<i∑​aij​.

The Butcher tableau arranges c, A, and b into one coefficient diagram. The number of stages s counts RHS evaluations in this implementation. Order p means a global error of O(h^p) under the required smoothness, stability, and fixed-interval assumptions. Stage predictors Y_i are generally not accurate solutions at intermediate times. Stage times can go backward: SSPRK(4,3) evaluates at c=(0,1/2,1,1/2). Time-dependent forcing and boundary data must use the correct c_i, not just the next chronological fraction of a step.

RK2 is a family#

The two-stage, second-order family can be written

k1=f(tn,yn),k2=f(tn+αh,yn+αhk1),yn+1=yn+h[(1−12α)k1+12αk2]. k_1=f(t_n,y_n),\quad k_2=f(t_n+\alpha h,y_n+\alpha h k_1),\quad y_{n+1}=y_n+h\left[\left(1-\frac1{2\alpha}\right)k_1+ \frac1{2\alpha}k_2\right]. k1​=f(tn​,yn​),k2​=f(tn​+αh,yn​+αhk1​),yn+1​=yn​+h[(1−2α1​)k1​+2α1​k2​].

Heun / explicit trapezoid takes alpha=1; explicit midpoint takes alpha=1/2; Ralston takes alpha=2/3. Heun is also SSPRK(2,2). For the full nonlinear methods here, their SSP coefficients are respectively 1, 0, and 1/2, as checked from the absolute-monotonicity inequalities for the complete tableau.

All three have the same scalar linear stability polynomial

R(z)=1+z+12z2,z=hλ. R(z)=1+z+\tfrac12 z^2,\qquad z=h\lambda. R(z)=1+z+21​z2,z=hλ.

Consequently their trajectories on y′=lambda y coincide in exact arithmetic. Their nonlinear stages and errors need not coincide. The notebook therefore also computes y′=−y², y(0)=1, whose exact solution is 1/(1+t). This separates methods that an oscillator alone cannot distinguish. One benchmark does not establish a universally best RK2 variant.

RK3, classical RK4, and SSP stage counts#

The implemented third-order group contains Kutta's classical three-stage method, SSPRK(3,3), and SSPRK(4,3). The first two share R(z)=1+z+z²/2+z³/6, but their nonlinear SSP coefficients differ: 0 and 1. SSPRK(4,3) uses a fourth RHS evaluation, has C=2, and adds z⁴/48 to that polynomial. Four stages do not make it fourth order.

Classical four-stage RK4 uses weights (1/6,1/3,1/3,1/6). Its stability polynomial adds z⁴/24 instead, and it is fourth order. Its general nonlinear SSP coefficient is zero. This does not say that every RK4 computation oscillates; it says the generic forward-Euler SSP inheritance guarantee is unavailable. The Numipedia RK4 record lists its tableau and scalar stability properties.

For comparison, the notebook implements the five-stage fourth-order Spiteri–Ruuth SSP method, labeled SSPRK(5,4). Its full coefficients are given in section 3.1 of the Gottlieb–Ketcheson–Shu review. The implementation converts that decimal Shu–Osher form to a Butcher tableau; representing increments relative to y_n avoids drift from rounded affine weights. It uses the conservative rounded bound C=1.508, rather than claiming an exact rational coefficient. Order conditions and nonlinear refinement are checked at the supplied coefficient precision.

For standard explicit RK methods with a positive general nonlinear SSP coefficient, the order is at most four. A four-stage fourth-order explicit method cannot have C>0; fourth-order SSP therefore needs additional stages. SSPRK(5,4) is one example, not a claim that every five-stage fourth-order method is SSP. See Spiteri–Ruuth (2002) and Gottlieb–Ketcheson–Shu (2009).

What SSP preserves#

Suppose a particular semidiscrete spatial operator L satisfies a convex stability property under forward Euler for h ≤ h_FE. An SSP method with coefficient C inherits that property under

h≤ChFE. h\le C h_{\rm FE}. h≤ChFE​.

The premise might concern total variation, a norm, or an admissible convex set, provided the actual operator and its stage treatment satisfy the premise. SSP does not create a TVD spatial discretization, cure an inappropriate boundary condition, or supply missing positive Euler states. The SSP review by Gottlieb–Shu–Tadmor (2001) develops this viewpoint. Compare it with the separate slope-limiter and positive-reconstruction requirements.

The family labels are SSPRK(s,p): stages first, order second. In this edition, Heun / SSPRK(2,2) has C=1, SSPRK(3,3) has C=1, SSPRK(4,3) has C=2, and SSPRK(5,4) has C=1.508 as a rounded bound. The SSP step bound per RHS evaluation, C/s, is useful context but is not a wall-clock performance ranking. Memory traffic, storage, spatial accuracy, and other stability constraints can decide the actual cost.

Linear stability is a separate picture#

For y′=lambda y, one step multiplies y by

R(z)=1+z bT(I−zA)−11. R(z)=1+z\,b^T(I-zA)^{-1}{\bf1}. R(z)=1+zbT(I−zA)−11.

The region |R(z)|≤1 is the scalar absolute-stability region. Its boundary is sampled on a 401×401 grid over Re(z)∈[−6,2], Im(z)∈[−4,4]. The curves in the notebook are approximate contours, not rigorous boundary locations. An eigenvalue of a normal linear spatial operator suggests where to evaluate R; eigenvalues alone may miss transient growth for a nonnormal operator.

For the undamped oscillator, RK2 has no nonzero imaginary-axis stability interval, while SSPRK(3,3) and classical RK4 do. This concerns the scalar linear problem; it does not contradict Heun's SSP property, because forward Euler itself has no nonzero stable step on that oscillator.

Other branches of the time-integration family#

Embedded RK pairs, such as Dormand–Prince 5(4), use two orders to estimate error and select a step. Here those parentheses describe the embedded orders, unlike the stage/order pair in SSPRK(s,p). No embedded error estimator, adaptive controller, dense output, or FSAL optimization runs in this edition.

Low-storage RK methods reorganize stage calculations to reduce stored arrays; they are not automatically SSP. Implicit RK families include midpoint and Gauss/Radau/SDIRK methods, with nonlinear solves and their own stability properties. IMEX pairs split explicit and implicit operators. These are reading context, except for the existing scalar linear backward-Euler and implicit-midpoint demonstrations. The generic rk_step below supports the nine explicit tableaus only. Leapfrog is a multistep recurrence; mechanical Verlet and the symplectic maps form another distinct branch.

Reproduction and verification#

from numart.runge_kutta import rk_step, nonlinear_trajectory

value, stages = rk_step(lambda t, y: -y*y, 0., 1., 0.5,
                        method="ssprk43", return_stages=True)
t, y = nonlinear_trajectory("ssprk54", steps=40)

python scripts/build_runge_kutta.py
python scripts/sync_web_content.py

The JSON records all tableaus, contours, 27 one-step stage cases (h=0.25,0.5,1), and nine refinement studies (N=10,20,40,80, T=1), plus source hashes. Errors use the exact nonlinear solution; stages are actual RHS evaluations. Tests check all order conditions through order four, a nonlinear manufactured time-dependent equation, complex linear stability factors, full-tableau SSP inequalities at the stated C, and scalar MUSCL conservation/TVD under the inherited step restriction. These do not turn the existing Euler/WENO solvers into selectable-RK or fully positivity-preserving implementations.
