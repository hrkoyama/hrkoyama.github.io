CIP: carry the value and the gradient

The original cubic-interpolated pseudo-particle (CIP) idea stores a field and its spatial derivative, then reconstructs an upwind cubic profile. The broader family is also called constrained interpolation profile. The CIP notebook implements the original polynomial construction for constant-speed periodic scalar advection. It compares value-only linear interpolation with CIP and checks diffusion, gradient transport, oscillations, refinement, and conservation.

Historical references are Takewaki–Nishiguchi–Yabe (1985) and Yabe–Aoki (1991). The accessible Saito–Obase–Abe-Ouchi (2020) paper describes CIP-family value/gradient evolution and a rational variant; its ice-sheet application and RCIP algorithm are separate from this polynomial example.

Characteristics and an additional unknown#

For constant velocity a,

ft+afx=0,g=fx,gt+agx=0. f_t+a f_x=0,\qquad g=f_x,\qquad g_t+a g_x=0. ft​+afx​=0,g=fx​,gt​+agx​=0.

At a receiving point x_i, trace backward to x_d=x_i−a dt. Interpolate both f and g from the old upwind interval at that departure point. The evolved g is an independent state variable; it is not replaced after each step by a finite difference of the updated f. For N nodes, CIP therefore stores 2N scalar unknowns, while the linear comparison stores N.

For variable velocity the gradient equation instead includes stretching:

gt+agx=−axg. g_t+a g_x=-a_xg. gt​+agx​=−ax​g.

Source terms, velocity variation, and compressibility require additional updates and characteristic treatment. A transported tracer f_t+a f_x=0 and a conserved density f_t+(af)_x=0 also differ when a_x is nonzero. This edition uses constant a only, where the two PDE forms coincide.

Four endpoint constraints determine a cubic#

On an interval of width h, with normalized coordinate s∈[0,1], the cubic is

P(s)=H00(s)fL+hH10(s)gL+H01(s)fR+hH11(s)gR, P(s)=H_{00}(s)f_L+hH_{10}(s)g_L+H_{01}(s)f_R+hH_{11}(s)g_R, P(s)=H00​(s)fL​+hH10​(s)gL​+H01​(s)fR​+hH11​(s)gR​,
H00=2s3−3s2+1,H10=s3−2s2+s,H01=−2s3+3s2,H11=s3−s2. \begin{aligned} H_{00}&=2s^3-3s^2+1,&H_{10}&=s^3-2s^2+s,\\ H_{01}&=-2s^3+3s^2,&H_{11}&=s^3-s^2. \end{aligned} H00​H01​​=2s3−3s2+1,=−2s3+3s2,​H10​H11​​=s3−2s2+s,=s3−s2.​

The endpoint values and physical derivatives are reproduced, and the new states are f_i=P(s_d) and g_i=P′(s_d)/h. Here P′ differentiates with respect to s. For positive a, the interval is [x_{i−1},x_i] and s_d=1−a dt/h; for negative a it is [x_i,x_{i+1}] and s_d=−a dt/h. The implementation restricts |a|dt/h≤1 to keep the departure point in this adjacent interval. The case |a|dt/h=1 shifts both arrays by one node exactly.

This is a complete characteristic/remapping update for this constant-speed problem, rather than a spatial RHS subsequently advanced by an arbitrary RK method. It belongs beside the RK family, not inside its list of time-integration tableaus. More general semi-Lagrangian schemes can cross multiple cells; that capability is outside this adjacent-cell example.

Reproducible scalar tests#

The domain is periodic [0,1], with N=128 point nodes x_i=i/N. Notebook choices are a=±1, Courant magnitudes 0.25, 0.6, or 1, and final times 0.25 or 1. The last step is shortened. Every configuration is solved independently to its selected final time, without interpolation between saved solutions.

The smooth profile is 1+0.2 sin(2πx). Initial g is either its analytic derivative or the centered periodic difference. The square pulse is one on [0.2,0.4) and zero elsewhere. Its initial slopes are either zero or centered differences. Zero is an explicit convention at a sampled discontinuity, not an analytic derivative there. Consequently there is no square-pulse exact gradient error displayed.

The reference f is exact translated point samples. These are not the cell-average references used in the finite-volume limiter notebook. Reported L1 means the average absolute point error. Initial-gradient choice can materially affect sharp features. The basic cubic has no TVD, monotonicity, or positivity limiter, and the square test shows overshoot and undershoot. The linear interpolation comparison is the same signed-characteristic point-value update, algebraically first-order upwind for |C|≤1.

Smooth refinement uses N=32,64,128,256, a=1, Courant=0.6, and T=0.37. The computed value errors approach order three for both gradient initializations; the gradient errors are recorded separately. A cubic interpolant alone is not a fourth-order global-accuracy claim after repeated remapping. Integer Courant shifts are a special exact-translation case, so they are not used to infer a generic refinement order. No equal-cost ranking is made between the N-state linear method and the 2N-state CIP method.

Why a sine wave is not a conservation proof#

The exact integral of the periodic piecewise-Hermite reconstruction is

M=∑i[h2(fi+fi+1)+h212(gi−gi+1)]=h∑ifi. M=\sum_i\left[\frac h2(f_i+f_{i+1})+ \frac{h^2}{12}(g_i-g_{i+1})\right]=h\sum_i f_i. M=i∑​[2h​(fi​+fi+1​)+12h2​(gi​−gi+1​)]=hi∑​fi​.

For uniform constant-speed remapping with fraction s, its one-step change is

ΔM=h2 [H10(s)+H11(s)]∑igi. \Delta M=h^2\,[H_{10}(s)+H_{11}(s)]\sum_i g_i. ΔM=h2[H10​(s)+H11​(s)]i∑​gi​.

The initial analytic sine slopes and centered differences sum to zero, so the displayed scalar runs can conserve this integral to roundoff even though the method has no universal conservative remapping property.

The separate counterexample uses N=32 and a valid periodic C1 Hermite field with f_i=1 and g_i=1 at every node. This is not a constant function: each interval contains a small cubic ripple, and its derivative varies between the nodes. Its exact integral is 1. Exact translation preserves that integral, while one CIP remapping step at C=0.35 gives

s=0.65,ΔM=h s(1−s)(1−2s)=−0.0021328125. s=0.65,\qquad \Delta M=h\,s(1-s)(1-2s)=-0.0021328125. s=0.65,ΔM=hs(1−s)(1−2s)=−0.0021328125.

The plot shows the exact shifted original reconstruction and the reconstructed CIP field over the first four intervals. This is an intentionally constructed grid-scale test of a claimed property, not a prediction of the mass error for every smooth flow. Integrals are evaluated analytically, not by coarse quadrature. Small errors in an easy test cannot establish a stronger guarantee.

Conservative and shape-preserving branches#

CIP–CSL (Yabe et al., 2001) introduces an integral constraint and conservative semi-Lagrangian transport. Rational CIP / RCIP changes the reconstruction, and variants apply additional shape constraints. Conservation and boundedness require their own definitions and tests; neither follows solely from the CIP name.

This edition implements the original 1D cubic only. CIP–CSL2/CSL4, RCIP, fluid advection/non-advection splitting, multidimensional mixed derivatives, and complete compressible or multiphase solvers are reading context. The existing Euler shock-tube solvers retain their documented reconstruction and flux choices.
