Benchmark library

For the representation behind DG and spectral examples, see orthogonal polynomials and element bases: Legendre modes, nodal transforms, quadrature, and their mass matrices.

Each 1D Problem stores the equation, domain, final time, boundaries, initial condition, reference solution, and citation keys. get_problem(name) exposes the same definition to every method. The last array axis contains variables: Euler uses (ρ,ρu,E), shallow water uses (h,hu), and isothermal flow uses (ρ,ρu).

Smooth problems#

Linear advection uses periodic [0,1], speed a, and initial data sin(2πx). The exact translated solution compares finite differences, DG, and Fourier methods. DG uses modal Legendre P0/P1/P2, upwind boundary fluxes, and SSPRK3. Distinguish the spatial order of Pp from the third-order time integrator. DG has elements × (p+1) unknowns. Fourier integrates the linear modes exactly in time; the gallery does not establish an equal-cost or equal-degree-of-freedom ranking. Nonlinear products, dealiasing, and Gibbs treatments are future work.

entropy-wave uses Euler data ρ=1+0.2sin(2πx), u=1,p=1,γ=1.4, periodic [0,1], and t=1. Only density translates, giving an exact reference for smooth accuracy and periodic conservation. Isothermal Euler has no independent energy or entropy variable and does not support this same Euler entropy-wave problem.

Shock tubes#

Sod: [0,1], x0=0.5, left (ρ,u,p)=(1,0,1), right (0.125,0,0.1), γ=1.4, and t=0.2. Boundaries use zero-gradient extrapolation; the main waves do not reach them before the final time. The exact Riemann solver finds the matching pressure by bisection and samples shocks, contacts, and rarefaction fans. Exact solutions containing a vacuum are unsupported. Sod (1978), exact-solution implementation reference.

Lax: [-5,5], x0=0, left (0.445,0.698,3.528), right (0.5,0,0.571), γ=1.4, t=1.3, and extrapolation boundaries. The Lax test and the Lax–Friedrichs algorithm are different namesakes. These are standard parameters from later literature; the original 1954 paper's table has not been directly matched here.

Shu–Osher: [-5,5], x0=-4, t=1.8, γ=1.4, left (3.857143,2.629369,10.33333), and right (1+0.2sin(5x),0,1). Ghost states are fixed to the initial conditions evaluated at the domain ends. There is no exact final-time reference; the first-edition figure compares MUSCL and WENO numerical results. Shu–Osher (1989), adopted parameters in Quokka, §3.1.5.

Low densities and Einfeldt-type tests#

double-rarefaction uses [0,1], x0=0.5, left (1,-2,0.4), right (1,2,0.4), γ=1.4, and t=0.15. This strong expansion is commonly called the 1–2–3 problem. The selected positivity test uses piecewise-constant reconstruction with HLLE. It does not transfer a positivity guarantee to high-order MUSCL or WENO. Einfeldt et al. (1991).

Shallow-water and isothermal flow#

dam-break solves flat-bottom shallow water with g=1, left h=2, right h=1, u=0, [0,1], and t=0.1. Dry beds, topographic sources, well-balanced discretization, and wetting/drying are unsupported. isothermal uses p=cs²ρ, cs=1, left ρ=1, right ρ=0.125, and u=0 on the same domain and time interval. Both use extrapolation boundaries. First-edition checks compare solvers, admissible states, and conservative form. The reference library now supplies semi-analytic Riemann states and conserved cell means for the equation gallery. The original Problem.reference callbacks for these two cases remain unset; use get_reference for the new evaluations. LeVeque (2002).

Riemann fluxes and reconstruction#

Rusanov and HLL support Euler, shallow water, and isothermal flow. HLLC, Roe, and HLLE currently support ideal-gas Euler only. HLL uses minimum/maximum local acoustic speeds; HLLE uses Einfeldt-type estimates including Roe averages. Roe applies quadratic entropy smoothing to acoustic eigenvalues with δ=0.1ã, leaving the contact eigenvalue unchanged. This is neither a low-density positivity fix nor a general entropy-stable flux construction.

MUSCL uses selectable minmod (default), Superbee, MC, van Leer, or clipped van Albada reconstruction in primitive variables; see limiters. WENO5-JS is a componentwise finite-difference implementation with pointwise fluxes and global LF splitting. A separate reconstruction chapter below adds characteristics and WENO-Z; this original solver remains unchanged. Fully discrete positivity preservation is still future work. A separate advanced limiter exhibit implements TVB modified minmod and positive Euler reconstruction scaling; neither is coupled to these Euler time updates. Errors are labeled pointwise: finite-volume cell averages and finite-difference point values are not strictly interchangeable.

See MHD for Orszag–Tang definitions and limits, and elliptic problems for diffusion and Poisson cases.

Matching the benchmark to the scheme#

The original KEEP exhibit uses a smooth isentropic vortex and conservation diagnostics. Its bare central flux has no shock stabilization and is not presented as a Sod/DMR solver. The movie guide separates those smooth computations from shock-capturing and MHD movies, and keeps steady fields as still images.

Equation-first scalar comparisons#

The equation gallery adds Burgers and LWR traffic Riemann problems with exact cell-integrated references, plus advection and heat mode tests. It also connects the existing Sod, wet dam-break and isothermal baselines, with each reference convention explicitly labeled.

Expanded reconstruction and flux tests#

The new reconstruction chapter and interactive comparison add limited parabolic FV, conservative characteristics, FD WENO-Z and an explicit AUSM-family baseline. The original code described above remains a componentwise baseline. Planar shock sensitivity and Roe's Property U have separate verification.
