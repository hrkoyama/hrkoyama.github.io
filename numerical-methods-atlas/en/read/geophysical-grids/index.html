Spherical meshes and Arakawa A, B and C grids

A/B/C names specify where different variables live on a mesh. They are not an accuracy order, a mesh shape, or a time-stepping family. A structured rectangular mesh may use any of these staggerings. The conventional comparison is described by Arakawa–Lamb (1977), with a useful agency exposition in the ECMWF numerical-methods notes.

Implemented here: spherical mesh geometry diagrams, plus placement diagrams and tested Fourier symbols for a linear periodic f-plane shallow-water system. This is not an A/B/C nonlinear weather or ocean model, and it introduces no new full flow simulation.

A sphere needs several charts#

Mesh geometry and variable staggering are separate choices. A cubed sphere can carry a C-grid arrangement; a triangular mesh can also store edge-normal velocities. A/B/C labels alone do not specify spherical connectivity or metrics.

On a sphere of radius R, with latitude φ and longitude λ,

ds2=R2(dφ2+cos⁡2φ dλ2),Δsλ≃Rcos⁡φ Δλ. ds^2=R^2\left(d\varphi^2+\cos^2\varphi\,d\lambda^2\right), \qquad \Delta s_\lambda\simeq R\cos\varphi\,\Delta\lambda. ds2=R2(dφ2+cos2φdλ2),Δsλ​≃RcosφΔλ.

Geographic poles are regular physical locations. Longitude becomes undefined there, while equal longitude increments become very short near them. An explicit wave/advection CFL bound can therefore become restrictive. Pole filters, reduced grids and spectral methods are other responses; replacing the mesh is not the only option. The three constructions below avoid the single latitude–longitude chart's polar convergence, while introducing other coupling and metric questions. None is automatically equal-area.

Yin–Yang: two overlapping low-latitude patches#

Kageyama–Sato (2004) combine two identical latitude–longitude patches. In each patch's own coordinates,

∣φ∣≤π/4+δ,∣λ∣≤3π/4+δ,xYang=(−100001010)xYin. |\varphi|\leq\pi/4+\delta,\qquad |\lambda|\leq3\pi/4+\delta, \qquad \boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{Yang}}= \begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix} \boldsymbol{x}^{\mathrm{Yin}}. ∣φ∣≤π/4+δ,∣λ∣≤3π/4+δ,xYang=​−100​001​010​​xYin.

The rotation puts each chart's poles inside the other chart's low-latitude region. The figure uses δ=3°; purple marks overlap, not a third patch. Each component retains orthogonal latitude–longitude coordinates and familiar metrics. Boundary data must be transferred through the overlap. Interpolating scalar values alone does not guarantee a globally conservative mass budget; overlap ownership/weights and conservative coupling need explicit design. Velocity components must also rotate between local tangent bases. These transfers are explained here, but no overset transport solver is implemented.

Cubed sphere: six connected panels#

The six-face construction is associated with Ronchi–Iacono–Paolucci (1996). Our diagram chooses equiangular gnomonic coordinates. For an orthonormal face frame with outward normal n and tangent directions e₁,e₂,

x(ξ,η)=R n+tan⁡ξ e1+tan⁡η e2∥n+tan⁡ξ e1+tan⁡η e2∥,−π/4≤ξ,η≤π/4. \boldsymbol{x}(\xi,\eta)=R\, \frac{\boldsymbol{n}+\tan\xi\,\boldsymbol{e}_1+\tan\eta\,\boldsymbol{e}_2} {\left\lVert\boldsymbol{n}+\tan\xi\,\boldsymbol{e}_1+\tan\eta\,\boldsymbol{e}_2\right\rVert}, \qquad -\pi/4\leq\xi,\eta\leq\pi/4. x(ξ,η)=R∥n+tanξe1​+tanηe2​∥n+tanξe1​+tanηe2​​,−π/4≤ξ,η≤π/4.

The picture has 8×8 cells per panel. Adjacent panel boundaries match; three panels meet at each cube corner. This mapping avoids a latitude–longitude pole but is generally nonorthogonal inside a face, and cell areas and metric factors vary. Other cubed-sphere projections have different properties. Neighbor indices, vector components and numerical fluxes must agree across seams. A continuous-looking diagram is not a test of those operators.

Icosahedral triangles and their dual#

Start with 20 triangular faces, split each triangle into four using edge midpoints, and project new vertices radially onto the sphere. At level L,

F=20 4L,E=30 4L,V=10 4L+2,V−E+F=2. F=20\,4^L,\qquad E=30\,4^L,\qquad V=10\,4^L+2, \qquad V-E+F=2. F=204L,E=304L,V=104L+2,V−E+F=2.

Level 2 has 320 triangles, 480 edges and 162 vertices. Twelve vertices have five neighbors; the others have six. Connecting neighboring face centers through shared edge midpoints produces a dual with twelve pentagons and otherwise hexagons. Here the centers are normalized triangle centroids: this is a barycentric dual, not a claim of a Voronoi/Delaunay orthogonal pair. For this unoptimized sample, the largest triangle area is about 1.275 times the smallest, so quasi-uniform does not mean equal-area. The connectivity formulas apply to this regular refinement, not every possible spherical unstructured mesh.

The ICON model description is a useful example of triangular mesh geometry combined with C-type staggering. Our illustration is not the optimized ICON mesh or its dynamical core. Accurate divergence, gradient, vorticity and wave dispersion still depend on compatible discrete operators.

Interfaces, vectors and what the figures verify#

A physical tangent velocity is one vector, even when its components differ between charts. If E_A and E_B have orthonormal tangent basis vectors as columns in one common Cartesian frame, then

v=EAuA=EBuB,uB=EBTEAuA. \boldsymbol{v}=E_A\boldsymbol{u}_A=E_B\boldsymbol{u}_B, \qquad \boldsymbol{u}_B=E_B^{\mathsf T}E_A\boldsymbol{u}_A. v=EA​uA​=EB​uB​,uB​=EBT​EA​uA​.

This formula uses orthonormal physical components. Covariant or contravariant components in nonorthogonal coordinates require the corresponding metric and dual basis. Copying two component arrays across a seam is insufficient. For finite volumes, a shared interface needs the same integrated flux with opposite signs in neighboring cell budgets. Overset grids additionally need a well-defined accounting of overlap; summing both full patches counts some area twice.

The geometry tests check unit radii, matching cube-panel boundary nodes, icosahedral edge incidence and valence, Euler's identity, and total spherical triangle area 4π. A deterministic equal-area sample checks Yin–Yang coverage and overlap; this sampled check is not a formal coverage proof. Reproduce both figures with python scripts/build_spherical_meshes.py; the geometry manifest records construction choices, area statistics and source/artifact hashes. These checks do not establish transport accuracy, seam conservation or geophysical wave convergence. Useful next tests would rotate a smooth tracer across every seam and monitor global mass, then compare balanced shallow-water waves under refinement.

A, B and C specify relative placement#

Take free-surface perturbation η at scalar cell centers (i+1/2,j+1/2), with square cell size Δ. Define u as the x velocity and v as the y velocity.

  • A grid: η, u and v are all at the same center.
  • B grid: η is at the center; both u and v are at vertices (i,j).
  • C grid: η is at the center; u is at x-normal faces (i,j+1/2), and v is at y-normal faces (i+1/2,j).

Choosing vertices rather than centers as the origin reverses some diagram conventions without changing the relative placement. Plot the locations and component directions before comparing array indices between models.

On a C grid, normal face velocities naturally supply cell divergence, and center-to-center scalar differences give face pressure gradients. That is the placement already used by the atlas's periodic MAC projection. The relationship concerns spatial location; MAC and an ocean C-grid dynamical core need not share their Coriolis, advection, boundary or time algorithms. The official MITgcm staggering description shows this face-normal arrangement in three dimensions.

Rotation and gravity waves make the difference visible#

For constant mean depth H and constant Coriolis parameter f,

ut−fv=−gηx,vt+fu=−gηy,ηt+H(ux+vy)=0. u_t-fv=-g\eta_x,\qquad v_t+fu=-g\eta_y, \qquad\eta_t+H(u_x+v_y)=0. ut​−fv=−gηx​,vt​+fu=−gηy​,ηt​+H(ux​+vy​)=0.

The continuum has a zero-frequency geostrophic branch and gravity branches

ω2=f2+c2(k2+ℓ2),c=gH,LD=c/∣f∣. \omega^2=f^2+c^2(k^2+\ell^2),\qquad c=\sqrt{gH},\qquad L_D=c/|f|. ω2=f2+c2(k2+ℓ2),c=gH​,LD​=c/∣f∣.

A discrete dispersion relation depends on the operators and the locations. Here each grid uses centered second-order pressure/divergence; C-grid Coriolis uses the symmetric four-point interpolation between u and v locations. With the physical staggering phases removed, the effective wavenumbers are

A:k~=sin⁡(kΔ)/Δ,ℓ~=sin⁡(ℓΔ)/Δ,fh=f,B:k~=2sin⁡(kΔ/2)cos⁡(ℓΔ/2)/Δ,ℓ~=2cos⁡(kΔ/2)sin⁡(ℓΔ/2)/Δ,fh=f,C:k~=2sin⁡(kΔ/2)/Δ,ℓ~=2sin⁡(ℓΔ/2)/Δ,fh=fcos⁡(kΔ/2)cos⁡(ℓΔ/2). \begin{aligned} A:\quad &\widetilde k=\sin(k\Delta)/\Delta, &\widetilde\ell=\sin(\ell\Delta)/\Delta,&\quad f_h=f,\\ B:\quad &\widetilde k=2\sin(k\Delta/2)\cos(\ell\Delta/2)/\Delta, &\widetilde\ell=2\cos(k\Delta/2)\sin(\ell\Delta/2)/\Delta,&\quad f_h=f,\\ C:\quad &\widetilde k=2\sin(k\Delta/2)/\Delta, &\widetilde\ell=2\sin(\ell\Delta/2)/\Delta,&\quad f_h=f\cos(k\Delta/2)\cos(\ell\Delta/2). \end{aligned} A:B:C:​k=sin(kΔ)/Δ,k=2sin(kΔ/2)cos(ℓΔ/2)/Δ,k=2sin(kΔ/2)/Δ,​ℓ=sin(ℓΔ)/Δ,ℓ=2cos(kΔ/2)sin(ℓΔ/2)/Δ,ℓ=2sin(ℓΔ/2)/Δ,​fh​=f,fh​=f,fh​=fcos(kΔ/2)cos(ℓΔ/2).​

The computed baseline has ω_h²=f_h²+c²(k̃²+ℓ̃²). For the energy-scaled state (u,v,cη/H), the full 3×3 Fourier generator is skew-Hermitian: its eigenvalues are 0 and ±iω_h. This is a semi-discrete result. A time integrator can add phase error, damping or instability even when the spatial generator conserves this linear energy.

Checkerboards and an unresolved deformation radius#

A centered A-grid pressure gradient cannot see the shortest alternating height mode in a coordinate direction. The B-grid baseline removes that axis-only null mode but retains a diagonal height checkerboard. The C-grid pressure-gradient/divergence pair couples that mode; however, its simple Coriolis average vanishes at certain shortest waves. These are distinct operator effects, not one universal checkerboard diagnosis.

The figure compares both axial and diagonal waves at L_D/Δ=0.25 and 2. A and B overlap on the diagonal for these square-grid operators, while their axial curves differ. When the deformation radius is unresolved, Coriolis averaging can strongly distort the C-grid near-inertial branches. Thus “C is always best” is not a valid conclusion. Resolved gravity waves, coarse-grid geostrophic adjustment and directional errors are different tests. Different Coriolis discretizations can change the comparison without changing the C placement itself.

D and E, vertical staggering and magnetic grids#

The D grid interchanges the face locations of u and v relative to C: u lies at the y-normal face location and v at the x-normal face location. The arrows still denote physical x and y components, so a D diagram is not obtained by merely renaming the C velocity arrays. The E grid is commonly viewed as a B arrangement rotated by 45 degrees; its indexing and resolvable wavevector set must be treated consistently. They are described here but have no implemented symbols in this addition.

Horizontal Arakawa staggering is separate from Lorenz versus Charney–Phillips vertical staggering. Likewise, MHD constrained transport uses face/edge placement to enforce a magnetic-divergence identity; it is not synonymous with an Arakawa C-grid shallow-water algorithm. Each needs its own compatible discrete operators.

Evidence and the next geophysical benchmarks#

numart.geophysical_grids supplies the A/B/C symbols. Tests compare them with independently applied physical-grid finite-difference stencils on Fourier waves, check skew-Hermitian energy structure and the geostrophic zero branch, verify long-wave second-order convergence, and expose the distinct short-wave null modes. Reproduce the figure with python scripts/build_geophysical_grids.py; the manifest records assumptions, sampled frequencies and source hashes.

A sensible next flow sequence is geostrophic adjustment, inertia–gravity waves, a coastal Kelvin wave, and a beta-plane Rossby wave, followed by potential-vorticity and nonlinear energy/enstrophy budgets. The present constant-f symbols do not verify beta-plane Rossby propagation, coastlines, spherical metrics, topography or wetting/drying. A nonlinear Arakawa Jacobian or energy/enstrophy-conserving shallow-water flux is another algorithmic choice; selecting A, B or C placement alone does not provide those invariants.
