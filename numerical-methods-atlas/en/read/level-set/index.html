Level sets: moving interfaces and comparison with VOF

A level set embeds a curve or surface as the zero contour of a grid function and evolves that function. It connects transport, Hamilton–Jacobi equations and interface geometry, with a representation that naturally accommodates merging and splitting. Start with the equations, then inspect small reproducible two-dimensional circle motions.

Represent the interface by phi=0

Γ(t)={x:ϕ(x,t)=0},ϕ<0  inside,ϕ>0  outside\Gamma(t)=\{\mathbf{x}:\phi(\mathbf{x},t)=0\},\qquad\phi<0\;\text{inside},\quad\phi>0\;\text{outside}Γ(t)={x:ϕ(x,t)=0},ϕ<0inside,ϕ>0outside

Here the interior is negative and the exterior positive. Function values are neither fluid density nor volume fraction. A monotone transformation retaining the zero set leaves the interface unchanged while changing gradients and numerical behavior. Some sources reverse the sign; normals and normal speeds must use the same convention.

An interface transported by a velocity

DϕDt=0⟹ϕt+u⋅∇ϕ=0\frac{D\phi}{Dt}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\phi_t+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\phi=0DtDϕ​=0⟹ϕt​+u⋅∇ϕ=0

Differentiate the condition that phi is constant along a trajectory moving with velocity u to obtain the transport equation. Prescribed-velocity interface transport differs from solving coupled two-phase velocities and pressure. Upwind differences, ENO/WENO and time integration discretize this equation; they are choices in addition to the interface representation.

Normals, curvature and normal motion

ϕt+Vn∥∇ϕ∥=0,n=∇ϕ∥∇ϕ∥,κ=∇⋅n\phi_t+V_n\|\nabla\phi\|=0,\qquad\mathbf{n}=\frac{\nabla\phi}{\|\nabla\phi\|},\quad\kappa=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}ϕt​+Vn​∥∇ϕ∥=0,n=∥∇ϕ∥∇ϕ​,κ=∇⋅n
ϕ(x)=sgn⁡(ϕ)dist⁡(x,Γ),∥∇ϕ∥=1where differentiable\phi(\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{sgn}(\phi)\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{x},\Gamma),\qquad\|\nabla\phi\|=1\quad\text{where differentiable}ϕ(x)=sgn(ϕ)dist(x,Γ),∥∇ϕ∥=1where differentiable

With a negative interior the normal points outward; the two-dimensional circle has kappa=1/r in this convention. A prescribed normal speed gives a Hamilton–Jacobi equation. A signed distance has unit gradient where differentiable; circle centers and points with nonunique nearest interfaces need not be smooth. Diagnostics therefore focus on a band near the interface; curvature-driven motion is explained but not executed here.

What redistancing repairs and can change

ψτ+Sϵ(ϕ0)(∥∇ψ∥−1)=0,ψ(x,0)=ϕ0(x),Sϵ(z)=zz2+ϵ2\psi_\tau+S_\epsilon(\phi_0)(\|\nabla\psi\|-1)=0,\quad\psi(\mathbf{x},0)=\phi_0(\mathbf{x}),\quad S_\epsilon(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2+\epsilon^2}}ψτ​+Sϵ​(ϕ0​)(∥∇ψ∥−1)=0,ψ(x,0)=ϕ0​(x),Sϵ​(z)=z2+ϵ2​z​

General advection need not retain a distance function. An auxiliary pseudo-time tau equation can restore distance quality without advancing physical time t. Preserving an ideal zero set does not guarantee an interpolated discrete interface stays fixed. The example uses first-order Godunov iterations with sign frozen per call; it is not the constrained higher-order Sussman–Fatemi scheme, and no position or area improvement is assumed.

For a prescribed incompressible velocity, volume fractions admit conservative transport. Cancellation of shared interior face fluxes gives a global balance with boundary fluxes. Bounds0<=F<=1 additionally depend on fluxes, reconstruction and CFL conditions; conservation alone is insufficient. Conserving phi itself does not conserve its enclosed area. The area diagnosed from piecewise-linear phi here is not a conservatively advected VOF fraction.

Small 2D examples: translation and off-center rotation

ϕref(x,t)=∥x−c(t)∥−r,ctr(t)=c0+tv,crot(t)=xc+R(ωt)(c0−xc)\phi_{\rm ref}(\mathbf{x},t)=\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{c}(t)\|-r,\quad \mathbf{c}_{\rm tr}(t)=\mathbf{c}_0+t\mathbf{v},\quad\mathbf{c}_{\rm rot}(t)=\mathbf{x}_c+R(\omega t)(\mathbf{c}_0-\mathbf{x}_c)ϕref​(x,t)=∥x−c(t)∥−r,ctr​(t)=c0​+tv,crot​(t)=xc​+R(ωt)(c0​−xc​)

Radius.15 on the unit square, T=1. Translation starts at(.3,.35) with velocity(.2,.1). Rotation carries a circle centered at(.68,.5) once about(.5,.5), at angular speed2pi. ENO2 one-sided differences and Heun RK2 use32²/64²/128² grids and Courant number<=.35. Analytic ghost values are prescribed at every stage while all interior values are numerically updated. These are prescribed boundaries, not periodic conditions.

Both cases compare advection alone against five redistancing pseudo-steps every.1 physical time. Observe0/.25/.5/.75/1; the rotation checks intermediate displacement as well as full-turn return.

What the error measurements mean

EΓ=max⁡t∈{.25,.5,.75,1}max⁡z∈Zh(t)∣∥z−c(t)∥−r∣,pobs=log⁡2 ⁣(EΓ(h)/EΓ(h/2))E_\Gamma=\max_{t\in\{.25,.5,.75,1\}}\max_{\mathbf{z}\in Z_h(t)}\big|\|\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{c}(t)\|-r\big|,\quad p_{\rm obs}=\log_2\!\left(E_\Gamma(h)/E_\Gamma(h/2)\right)EΓ​=t∈{.25,.5,.75,1}max​z∈Zh​(t)max​​∥z−c(t)∥−r​,pobs​=log2​(EΓ​(h)/EΓ​(h/2))

Z_h contains linearly interpolated grid-edge zero crossings; E_Gamma is the maximum radial error at these samples, not a continuous-interface Hausdorff distance. Geometric area clips linear phi on triangles and compares against pi*r², retaining initial geometric bias. Coupled grid/time refinement of advection alone observes about1.90 for translation and2.02 for rotation. SeparateN64 CFL-halving sensitivity is recorded; this is not a proof of pure spatial order.

Finest-grid worst relative area error changes about0.22% to0.93% for translation and1.06% to2.02% for rotation, from advection alone to redistancing. The simple redistancing increases error in these fixed runs. Distance-gradient quality, zero-interface accuracy and enclosed-volume balance are distinct diagnostics.

Scope and related reading

This release transports circles in prescribed velocities. It does not validate topology changes, curvature flow, surface tension or two-phase flow, nor benchmark VOF performance. Downloads retain analytic reference formulas, full2D final fields, intermediate interface samples, CSV, input hashes, all decisions and each redistancing area change. The existing Zalesak slotted-disk material is a separate conservative scalar-transport example; its code is not relabeled as a VOF implementation.

Primary publications, supporting sources and review scope

Distinguish original research, later variants, author monographs, pedagogical articles and official references. Bibliographic verification differs from consulting selected passages; neither implies a full-paper audit or numerical reproduction. Access-depth notes accompany each source.

  1. S. Osher and J. A. Sethian (1988). Fronts propagating with curvature-dependent speed: Algorithms based on Hamilton-Jacobi formulations. Journal of Computational Physics

    Research publication: proposal or related formulation · Selected source passages consulted · Consulted record

    Selected author-hosted original passages: abstract, implicit front representation and Hamilton–Jacobi transport/normal-motion formulation. No full-paper audit or reproduction of its full experiment suite.

  2. C. W. Hirt and B. D. Nichols (1981). Volume of fluid (VOF) method for the dynamics of free boundaries. Journal of Computational Physics

    Research publication: proposal or related formulation · Abstract and bibliography checked · Consulted record

    Publisher bibliography and abstract for the original volume-fraction formulation. No full-text algorithm audit or implementation of its donor–acceptor scheme.

  3. M. Sussman and E. Fatemi (1999). An Efficient, Interface-Preserving Level Set Redistancing Algorithm and Its Application to Interfacial Incompressible Fluid Flow. SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing

    Research publication: proposal or related formulation · Abstract and bibliography checked · Consulted record

    Official abstract and bibliography: redistancing quality includes retaining the zero interface; its constrained higher-order algorithm is not the simple Godunov auxiliary iteration implemented here.

  4. M. Sussman and E. G. Puckett (2000). A Coupled Level Set and Volume-of-Fluid Method for Computing 3D and Axisymmetric Incompressible Two-Phase Flows. Journal of Computational Physics

    Research publication: proposal or related formulation · Selected source passages consulted · Consulted record

    Author-hosted pp.302–305, equations(1)–(6),(9),(12),(15): signed-distance geometry, enclosed-mass issue and volume-fraction conservation. Sign is reversed here to make phi negative inside. No CLSVOF or two-phase fluid experiment executed.
