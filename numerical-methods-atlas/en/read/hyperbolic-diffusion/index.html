Nishikawa's hyperbolic diffusion: iterate a first-order system

Hiroaki Nishikawa (2007) introduces the first-order hyperbolic-system approach to steady diffusion, including second-order residual-distribution schemes. The 2010 extension unifies advection and diffusion. The date is the journal publication year, not the DOI's year.

Implemented here: a periodic 1D, first-order upwind finite-volume baseline with SSPRK2. It demonstrates equivalent steady equations; it does not implement the original paper's second-order multidimensional RD constructions.

Evolve the solution and its gradient#

For the steady problem −νu_xx=f, introduce p and pseudo-time τ:

∂τu−ν∂xp=f,∂τp−1Tr∂xu=−pTr. \partial_\tau u-\nu\partial_x p=f,\qquad \partial_\tau p-\frac1{T_r}\partial_x u=-\frac p{T_r}. ∂τ​u−ν∂x​p=f,∂τ​p−Tr​1​∂x​u=−Tr​p​.

At convergence, p=u_x, recovering the original steady diffusion equation. The homogeneous flux Jacobian has eigenvalues

λ±=±ν/Tr. \lambda_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\nu/T_r}. λ±​=±ν/Tr​​.

The relaxation time T_r is a numerical parameter controlling pseudo-time propagation and damping. It is not the physical diffusivity. A hyperbolic Courant restriction scales with h for fixed T_r; an explicit source treatment also has a relaxation restriction. The demonstration therefore uses

Δτ=Ca/h+1/Tr,a=ν/Tr,C≤12. \Delta\tau=\frac{C}{a/h+1/T_r},\qquad a=\sqrt{\nu/T_r},\quad C\leq\frac12. Δτ=a/h+1/Tr​C​,a=ν/Tr​​,C≤21​.

A physical heat equation is not recovered at every finite pseudo-time. Replacing the heat gallery's physical-time curve by this iteration history would change the problem. Time-accurate extensions require additional analysis.

Residual convergence and discretization error#

The manufactured steady reference is sin(2πx), with zero mean on the periodic unit interval and forcing ν(2π)²sin(2πx). The initial mean fixes the constant null mode. Both equation residuals are measured; the gradient residual is scaled by 2π in the combined RMS diagnostic. A small residual does not eliminate the first-order upwind spatial error. N=32,64,128 checks that error separately. The manifest records steps and pseudo-time sizes, without a matched-cost speedup claim.

Compare Thomas and multigrid, sparse preconditioning, and the physical diffusion gallery. Anisotropic diffusion, high-order face reconstruction, viscous Navier–Stokes coupling and the complete RD formulation are future extensions.
