01Galaxies & stellar systems
TITLE DISPLAYGalactic DynamicsJames Binney & Scott TremaineOpen Library ↗

Galactic Dynamics

James Binney & Scott Tremaine

Princeton University Press · Second edition (2008)

Reference ISBN
9780691130279

Connect stellar orbits, gravitational potentials, equilibrium, stability and encounters to the structure of galaxies and star clusters.

Preparation
Mechanics, vector calculus, ordinary differential equations, distribution functions and probability.
Our suggested route
Start with virial balance and gravitational equilibrium; for star clusters, follow encounters into relaxation and distribution-function evolution.

02Radiation & transfer
TITLE DISPLAYRadiative Processes in AstrophysicsGeorge B. Rybicki & Alan P. Lightman

Radiative Processes in Astrophysics

George B. Rybicki & Alan P. Lightman

Wiley · Original Rybicki–Lightman text; Wiley-VCH reprint (2024)

Reference ISBN
9783527414499

Follow radiative transfer into synchrotron emission, Compton scattering and atomic radiation, connecting emission mechanisms to observed spectra.

Preparation
Electromagnetism, special relativity, basic statistical and quantum mechanics, and calculus.
Our suggested route
Use blackbody radiation and Thomson scattering as a starting point, then investigate the formulas behind synchrotron emission and the SZ effect.

03Radiation & fluid conservation laws
TITLE DISPLAYFoundations of Radiation HydrodynamicsDimitri Mihalas & Barbara Weibel-MihalasOpen Library ↗

Foundations of Radiation Hydrodynamics

Dimitri Mihalas & Barbara Weibel-Mihalas

Dover Publications · Original 1984 text; Dover reprint (1999)

Reference ISBN
9780486409252

Bring fluid mass, momentum and energy conservation together with radiative transfer. Explore how the fluid equations change when radiation exerts forces and transports energy.

Preparation
Fluid mechanics, thermodynamics, radiative transfer, partial differential equations and introductory relativity.
Our suggested route
Review ordinary fluid conservation, then add radiation energy flux and pressure. Use mean free paths, diffusion and the Eddington limit as starting points for consulting this advanced reference.

04Stellar structure & evolution
TITLE DISPLAYStellar Structure and EvolutionRudolf Kippenhahn, Alfred Weigert & Achim WeissOpen Library ↗

Stellar Structure and Evolution

Rudolf Kippenhahn, Alfred Weigert & Achim Weiss

Springer · Second edition (2012)

Reference ISBN
9783642302558

Combine hydrostatic balance, energy generation, radiative transport and convection to study stellar interiors and evolution on the H–R diagram, moving from global estimates to structure calculations.

Preparation
Thermodynamics, calculus and ordinary differential equations, and basic radiation and ideal-gas physics.
Our suggested route
Move from virial balance and mass–luminosity scaling to the stellar structure equations. After Lane–Emden and Hayashi tracks, explore convection and the connection between pre-main-sequence and main-sequence evolution.

05Expansion & cosmological observations

P. J. E. Peebles

Princeton University Press · Original 1993 text; 2020 reprint

Reference ISBN
9780691209814

Study cosmic expansion, spacetime geometry, cosmological observations and the origin of galaxies and large-scale structure in a unified physical framework.

Preparation
Calculus, mechanics, thermodynamics and statistical mechanics, and introductory relativity.
Our suggested route
First distinguish redshift, distance and expansion rate. For a focused study of structure formation, continue with the next Peebles volume.

06Fluctuations & structure formation
TITLE DISPLAYThe Large-Scale Structure of the UniverseP. J. E. PeeblesOpen Library ↗

The Large-Scale Structure of the Universe

P. J. E. Peebles

Princeton University Press · Original 1980 text; 2020 reprint

Reference ISBN
9780691209838

Explore the gravitational growth and spatial statistics of fluctuations in an expanding universe, as a focused companion to the broader cosmology text.

Preparation
Fluid mechanics, differential equations, Fourier analysis, probability and statistics.
Our suggested route
Connect Jeans analysis to expansion, then study linear growth, Zel’dovich dynamics and halo statistics. Distinguish classical theory from current observational parameters.

S. Chandrasekhar

Learn how linearized disturbances, boundary conditions and eigenvalues determine stability, including the effects of rotation and magnetic fields.
