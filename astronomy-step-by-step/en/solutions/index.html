Solution library

Compare astrophysical solutions by assumptions, boundary data, mathematical form, and validity.

The same equations can yield different solutions when initial or boundary data change. This index connects named solutions to the assumptions and data that select them.

Exact, similarity, approximate, and numerical describe different aspects of a solution. A similarity profile can require numerical integration. Existence of an equilibrium also differs from its stability.

Lane–Emden equation and polytropes

Analytic special cases and numerical solutions

ξ⁻² d(ξ² dθ/dξ)/dξ = −θⁿ

Spherical hydrostatic equilibrium with a polytropic law P = Kρ¹⁺¹⁄ⁿ.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Integrate from θ(0) = 1 and θ′(0) = 0; use the first zero as the surface for finite-radius models.
Free parameters
Polytropic index n, K, and central density ρc.
Validity and caveats
Closed forms exist for n = 0, 1, 5. A finite surface does not exist for every n.

Bonnor–Ebert spheres and a cloud’s limiting mass

Numerical equilibrium sequence

ξ⁻² d(ξ² dψ/dξ)/dξ = e−ψ

Spherical isothermal self-gravity, without rotation or magnetic fields, truncated by external pressure.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Regular centre ψ = ψ′ = 0; match the surface gas pressure to the external pressure.
Free parameters
Sound speed, central density, and dimensionless truncation radius.
Validity and caveats
Computing equilibria and testing their stability are distinct. Critical values belong to the stated isothermal, pressure-confined problem.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Bonnor (1956) · Boyle’s law and gravitational instability ↗
Gravitational equilibriumLesson available

Ostriker’s isothermal cylinder and critical line mass

Exact analytic equilibrium

ρ(r) = ρc[1 + r²/(8H²)]−2,　H² = cₛ²/(4πGρc)

Infinite, axisymmetric, isothermal cylinder with no rotation or magnetic field.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Finite density and zero gradient on the axis; optional truncation at radius R by external pressure.
Free parameters
Sound speed, central density, and truncated line-mass fraction.
Validity and caveats
The limiting line mass is 2cₛ²/G. Total mass of an infinite filament and finite-filament ends are different issues.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Ostriker (1964) · The equilibrium of polytropic and isothermal cylinders, §III, equations (56)–(61) ↗
Gravitational equilibriumLesson available

Gravitational potentials of ellipsoids

Exact potential of a homogeneous ellipsoid

Φ = πGρ(A₁x² + A₂y² + A₃z²) + constant

A prescribed homogeneous ellipsoid; first compute its interior gravitational field.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Finite interior field and continuous matching to the exterior potential; axis integrals determine the coefficients.
Free parameters
Semiaxes a, b, c and density.
Validity and caveats
An arbitrarily prescribed ellipsoid need not be a rotating fluid equilibrium. See the tensor-virial chapter for equilibrium sequences.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Fitzpatrick · Hydrostatics: ellipsoidal potentials and rotating figures ↗
Gravitational equilibriumLesson available

Tensor virial theorem and Maclaurin/Jacobi sequences

Analytic conditions and numerical equilibrium sequences

Maclaurin: a = b;　Jacobi: a > b > c

Homogeneous incompressible self-gravitating fluid in uniform rotation with a free surface.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Zero surface pressure; total gravitational and centrifugal potential is constant on the surface.
Free parameters
Axis ratios, angular speed, and density.
Validity and caveats
Equilibrium bifurcation, secular instability, and dynamical instability differ. Do not directly apply incompressible thresholds to compressible stars.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Lebovitz (1961) · The virial tensor and rotating figures ↗

Bondi accretion and the sonic point

Steady transonic solution

Ṁ = 4πr²ρv;　rₛ = GM/(2cₛ²)　(isothermal)

Steady spherical inflow dominated by a central mass; the panel is isothermal.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Specify far-field density and sound speed; select the inflow branch passing smoothly through the sonic point.
Free parameters
Central mass, ambient density, and sound speed.
Validity and caveats
Omit rotation, magnetic fields, and radiative feedback. Other stationary branches need not cross the sonic point.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Bondi (1952) · On spherically symmetrical accretion ↗
Flows and similarityLesson available

Parker wind and transonic outflow

Steady transonic solution

(v − cₛ²/v) dv/dr = 2cₛ²/r − GM/r²

Steady spherical isothermal outflow; the mechanism maintaining temperature is supplied.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Join an inner subsonic flow to an outer supersonic flow through the sonic point; supply density normalization separately.
Free parameters
Central mass, sound speed, and mass flux or reference density.
Validity and caveats
Similar equations to Bondi, but different branches and boundary conditions. Omit the actual solar wind’s thermal and magnetic structure.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Parker (1958) · Dynamics of the interplanetary gas and magnetic fields ↗

Sedov–Taylor blast waves

First-kind similarity; numerically integrated profiles

R(t) = β(Et²/ρ₀)1/5

Point explosion into uniform gas with an ideal equation of state and a strong adiabatic shock.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Shock jumps at the outer boundary, interior regularity, and total-energy normalization.
Free parameters
Explosion energy, ambient density, adiabatic index, and time.
Validity and caveats
The radius scaling alone does not determine the profiles. Excludes ejecta-dominated and strongly radiative stages.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Ohio State · Spherical blastwaves and supernova remnants ↗
Flows and similarityLesson available

Larson–Penston and Shu isothermal collapse

Numerical similarity solutions

x = r/(cₛt),　ρ = α(x)/(4πGt²),　Ṁ = m₀cₛ³/G

Spherical isothermal self-gravity; displayed profiles are after central formation.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Shu collapse joins a singular isothermal envelope; LPH is a distinct continuation with a moving envelope.
Free parameters
Sound speed, time, and the family specified by outer density and velocity.
Validity and caveats
Identical similarity variables do not imply identical accretion coefficients. Rounded published LPH outer coefficients are supplied inputs.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Shu (1977) · Self-similar collapse of isothermal spheres ↗
Flows and similarityLesson available

Lynden–Bell–Pringle viscous-disk similarity solutions

Analytic similarity for a prescribed viscosity

∂Σ/∂t = (3/r)∂[r1/2∂(νΣr1/2)/∂r]/∂r

Thin axisymmetric Keplerian disk with ν ∝ rᵞ and no external torque.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Zero torque at the origin, an infinite outer domain, and finite mass and angular momentum.
Free parameters
Viscosity exponent γ, initial mass and length scales, and dimensionless time.
Validity and caveats
Viscosity is prescribed. Does not directly solve MRI turbulence or angular-momentum loss in a wind.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Lynden-Bell & Pringle (1974) · The evolution of viscous discs ↗

Zel’dovich approximation and pancakes

First-order Lagrangian approximation

x(q,t) = q + D(t)s(q);　ρ/ρ̄ = 1/det(∂x/∂q)

Pressureless structure growth before shell crossing, in a single-stream description.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Supply the initial displacement field and growth factor.
Free parameters
Initial displacement amplitude and shape, and growth factor.
Validity and caveats
Not an exact solution of general three-dimensional gravitational evolution. Do not continue as a single stream after the Jacobian vanishes.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Zel’dovich (1970) · Gravitational instability: an approximate theory ↗

Strömgren spheres and ionization fronts

Photon-balance equilibrium and an idealized front

Rₛ³ = 3QH/(4παB nₑnₚ)

Uniform pure hydrogen, ionization-bounded radiation, and case-B recombination.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Supply the central ionizing-photon rate; at equilibrium it balances integrated recombinations.
Free parameters
Photon rate, density, and recombination coefficient.
Validity and caveats
Omit dust and photon leakage. Static ionization balance differs from later pressure-driven expansion.

Fokker–Planck equation

Analytic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck closure

∂f/∂t = γ∂(vf)/∂v + D∂²f/∂v²

One-dimensional linear drift, constant diffusion, and an initially Gaussian distribution.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Vanishing flux at infinite velocity and unit total probability.
Free parameters
γ, D, and initial mean and variance.
Validity and caveats
A benchmark, not a self-consistent cluster Fokker–Planck solution. Omits evolving gravity and transport coefficients.

Relativistic spacetimesLesson available

Schwarzschild: clocks, the photon sphere, and the ISCO

Exact vacuum Einstein solution

ds² = −(1−2rg/r)c²dt² + (1−2rg/r)−1dr² + r²dΩ²

Spherical vacuum, asymptotic flatness, and normalization by the central mass M.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Approach Minkowski spacetime far away and normalize t to a distant static clock.
Free parameters
M, with rg = GM/c². A test-particle orbit additionally needs conserved energy and angular momentum.
Validity and caveats
Excludes stellar interiors and rotation. Distinguish the coordinate problem at 2rg from the curvature singularity at r = 0.
Read the derivation and explore ↗Schwarzschild (1916) · Original paper, English translation ↗

Kerr: rotation, the ergoregion, and the ISCO

Exact vacuum Einstein solution

χ = cJ/(GM²)

Stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat rotating vacuum spacetime.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Normalize time at infinity and choose exterior timelike observers and orbits.
Free parameters
M, J, and orbital orientation.
Validity and caveats
Distinguish the horizon, static limit, and ISCO. The panel uses a subextremal exterior; it does not integrate trajectories or render a shadow.

Tomimatsu–Sato: Ernst potentials and the quadrupole

Exact vacuum family; lesson focuses on δ = 2

QTS2/M³ = −(1+3q²)/4

Stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat vacuum; δ = 1 is Kerr.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Supply the Ernst potential and examine asymptotic multipoles and singularities.
Free parameters
M, q = J/M², and integer δ; the panel compares δ = 1 and 2.
Validity and caveats
Do not identify δ > 1 with a regular black hole; examine ring singularities and causal structure.

Lin–Mestel–Shu: collapse of a uniform spheroid

Axis ODEs for homogeneous pressure-free flow

äᵢ = −2πGρAᵢaᵢ

Homogeneous nonrotating self-gravitating spheroid; no pressure or magnetic field.

Boundary data, parameters, and validity
Boundary / initial data
Supply initial axes and velocities; the panel starts at rest and stops before axis collapse.
Free parameters
Initial axis ratio q₀, density ρ₀, and time.
Validity and caveats
No post-caustic evolution; distinguish this model from isothermal Shu collapse and expanding-universe approximations.
