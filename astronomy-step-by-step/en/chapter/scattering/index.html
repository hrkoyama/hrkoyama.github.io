FOUNDATIONS · 05 / ATOMS & RADIATION

The classical electron radius and Thomson scattering

How a tiny effective area helps set a star’s luminosity limit.

Reading time: 15 minTools: Electric forces, energy, and averages
WHAT YOU WILL LEARNConnect the scale rₑ to σT and then to opacity κ.

01Compare electric and rest-energy scales

Define rₑ as the length at which the electric energy scale e²/(4πε₀r) equals the electron rest energy mₑc². Multiply by r, then divide by mₑc².

e24πε0re = mec2　⇒　re = e24πε0mec2 ≈ 2.82 × 10−15 m
This is not a measured physical radius of the electron. It is a length built from electromagnetic constants. A model of a charged sphere would have a self-energy coefficient depending on its charge distribution.

02Light shakes an electron, which radiates

Let the incident field be E(t) = E₀ cos(2πνt). A free electron has acceleration amplitude a₀ = eE₀/mₑ. Since the cycle average of cos² is 1/2, ⟨a²⟩ = e²E₀²/(2mₑ²).

⟨P⟩ = e2⟨a2⟩6πε0c3 = e4E0212πε0me2c3
We take the nonrelativistic Larmor radiation law as an input rather than deriving it here.

03Cross section = scattered power / incident flux

The mean incident flux is I = ε₀cE₀²/2. A scattering cross section is an effective area: it tells us how much incident power is redirected, rather than measuring a little solid ball.

σT = ⟨P⟩I = e46πε02me2c4 = 8π3re2
σT ≈ 6.65 × 10−29 m2
E₀² cancels. In this weak-field limit the cross section depends on neither intensity nor frequency.

04From one electron to one kilogram of gas

κe = neσTρ
ρ is mass density and nₑ electron number density. Opacity κ has units m²/kg.

For fully ionized pure hydrogen, one electron accompanies each proton, so ρ ≈ nₑmₚ and κₑ ≈ σT/mₚ ≈ 0.040 m²/kg. For a hydrogen–helium mixture with hydrogen mass fraction X, κₑ ≈ 0.020(1 + X) m²/kg, or about 0.034 at X ≈ 0.7.

Assumptions: nearly stationary free electrons, weak fields, and hν ≪ mₑc² ≈ 511 keV. At higher energies recoil matters; for bound electrons, resonances and absorption matter too.
CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Pause and work it out.

If the electron mass doubled while other constants stayed fixed, how would σT change?

Show the answer and reasoning

rₑ would halve. Since σT ∝ rₑ², the cross section would become one quarter as large.
