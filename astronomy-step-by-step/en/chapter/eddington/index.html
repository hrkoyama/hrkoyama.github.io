The Eddington limit: when light balances gravity

Use Thomson scattering to derive a characteristic luminosity limit.

Reading time: 15 minTools: Photon momentum and opacity
WHAT YOU WILL LEARNBalance forces and see why r² cancels, giving LEdd = 4πGMc/κ.

01Photons carry momentum as well as energy

pγ = Eγc,　f = L4πr2

Matter gains momentum by absorbing or scattering radiation. For Thomson scattering, angular averaging lets us use the flux and effective cross section for the net radial momentum transfer. Electric fields couple electrons to ions, so the gas moves together.

02Start with an electron–proton pair

Frad = σTL4πr2c,　Fgrav ≈ GMmpr2
M is the central mass; the surrounding gas is fully ionized pure hydrogen. Electron mass is negligible compared with proton mass.

03Equate the forces and cancel r²

σTL4πr2c = GMmpr2
σTL = 4πGMmpc　⇒　LEdd = 4πGMmpcσT

For one solar mass this is approximately 1.26 × 10³¹ W, or 3.3 × 10⁴ L☉, scaling linearly with M. We obtained the coefficient from a force balance without solving a differential equation.

04Use opacity for a different composition

arad = κL4πr2c,　agrav = GMr2
LEdd = 4πGMcκ
This balance assumes spherical symmetry, steady conditions, and a specified effective opacity. Disks, inhomogeneous gas, and transient events can exceed this luminosity. It is not an absolute cap on every astronomical source.
CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Pause and work it out.

At fixed κ, what happens to LEdd if the central mass grows by ten? What if radius doubles?

Show the answer and reasoning

Ten times the mass gives ten times the limiting luminosity. Radius cancels because both accelerations fall as 1/r².
