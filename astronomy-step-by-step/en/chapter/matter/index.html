Stars, gas, vacuum, and the forces of nature

Why does gravity govern stars when electromagnetism is so strong?

Reading time: 9 minTools: The inverse-square law
WHAT YOU WILL LEARNExplain charge cancellation and why gravity adds up.

01Is a cosmic vacuum really empty?

Space between stars contains atoms, ions, electrons, dust, and radiation. Even a very dilute medium can absorb or scatter light over a long path. Hot stellar interiors are plasmas: electrons are no longer bound to individual atoms.

02Compare two inverse-square laws

FG = Gmp2r2,　FE = e24πε0r2
FEFG = e24πε0Gmp2 ≈ 1.2 × 1036
e is the elementary charge, ε₀ the vacuum permittivity, and mₚ the proton mass. This ratio is for two protons.

03Large objects are nearly electrically neutral

Nearly equal positive and negative charges largely cancel the long-range electric force. Gravity from ordinary matter adds attractively. This makes gravity central to the global balance of stars, while magnetic fields and electromagnetic forces still matter in many cosmic settings.

04Pressure is force per area

P = FA,　Pgas = nkBT
n is the number of particles per unit volume. The ideal-gas equation describes pressure from particle motion.

Pressure itself has no direction. A star is supported by the force imbalance created when pressure is higher inside than outside.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Pause and work it out.

If two protons are separated twice as far, does the ratio of electric to gravitational force change?

Show the answer and reasoning

No. Both forces scale as 1/r², so the distance cancels in their ratio.
