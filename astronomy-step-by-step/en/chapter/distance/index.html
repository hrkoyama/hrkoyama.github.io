Measuring light, measuring distance

From the inverse-square law to the cosmic distance ladder.

Reading time: 12 minTools: Proportions and square roots
WHAT YOU WILL LEARNExplain how luminosity differs from apparent brightness and how light reveals distance.

01Light carries information

Radio waves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, and X-rays are all electromagnetic radiation. In vacuum they travel at c ≈ 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s. Wavelength λ and frequency ν obey c = λν. One photon carries E = hν = hc/λ.

E[eV] ≈ 1240λ[nm]
A 500 nm photon carries about 2.48 eV. Shorter wavelengths mean higher photon energies.

02Spread the light over a sphere

Luminosity L is the total energy a star emits per second. If it radiates uniformly, that energy crosses a sphere of area 4πd². The flux f is the power received per square metre.

f = L4πd2　⇒　d = √(L4πf)
This assumes no absorption and isotropic emission. Luminosity is in W; flux in W/m².

03Triangulation for nearby stars

As Earth orbits the Sun, a nearby star shifts against distant background objects. Its annual parallax p corresponds to a baseline of 1 au: half the maximum shift measured six months apart. One parsec is the distance corresponding to one arcsecond.

d[pc] = 1p[arcsec]
A parallax of 0.05 arcseconds gives 20 pc, about 65 light-years. A light-year is a distance.

04A ladder of calibrations

Solar-system ranging → stellar parallax → period–luminosity relations such as Cepheids → Type Ia supernovae. Nearby distances calibrate the intrinsic brightness used at the next stage. Type Ia supernovae must be standardized using their light-curve shapes and colours; their luminosities are not identical.

v ≈ H0d,　z ≈ vc
Approximations for the nearby universe at small redshift. H₀ must be calibrated using other distance measurements or a cosmological model.
At large redshift, d = cz/H₀ is insufficient. You need the expansion history, a cosmological model, and a specified definition of distance.

CHECK YOUR UNDERSTANDING

Pause and work it out.

Two stars have equal luminosity. One looks nine times fainter. How much farther away is it?

Show the answer and reasoning

Three times farther: f ∝ 1/d², so the distance ratio is √9 = 3. Equal luminosity is essential.
